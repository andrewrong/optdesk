# ADR 0003: Market-data architecture and IBKR access

Date: 2026-10-02
Status: accepted

## Context

Snapshots must cover a watchlist that may grow to 50–100 names. The names are in three tiers:
- **A, actionable:** a fast sell-zone pass at 09:45 New York time, then the rest; full selection before the close.
- **B, watch:** near-ATM contracts before the close.
- **C:** IV history only.

The owner trades by hand in Trader Workstation (TWS) or the mobile app after the open snapshot. The collector uses the owner's dedicated IB Gateway session.

Two advisor models debated the design over three rounds; the record is kept outside the repo. Both found, and the source confirms, that `ib_async.IB.connectAsync` requests positions, account updates and executions on connect and an account summary on reconnect, even with `readonly=True`. A library flag is not a guard.

## Decision

**Layers**
- The pure core is unchanged.
- Broker-independent ports (protocols) are added for definitions, quotes and history.
- Each source is an adapter package. Only `sources/ibkr` imports `ib_async`, and a test enforces this.
- `store.py` stays the only DuckDB writer.
- Every stored observation records:
  - its source;
  - the requested and actual market-data type;
  - its request, send and receive times;
  - its connection generation.

  A second data source, such as historical option data, adds an adapter and its own source tag. Data from different vendors is never silently mixed.

**IBKR connection**
- Do not use `IB.connectAsync`. A minimal connect starts the API and sets the market-data type, with nothing else.
- Every outbound message passes a guard on `Client.send`:
  - **Order messages are always refused.** These are place order, cancel order, global cancel and exercise options. This rule never changes.
  - **Every other message must be on an allowlist.** For now it holds market data, market-data type, contract data, option parameters, historical data, their cancels, and current time.
  - Account requests are not needed yet. A later issue may add them to the allowlist explicitly.
- Account identifiers that arrive during the handshake are not logged or stored.
- The IB Gateway "Read-Only API" setting stays on.
- The pinned `ib_async` version is re-audited on every upgrade.
- The collector connects to the owner's dedicated IB Gateway session: port from configuration, nonzero client id. Gateway auto-restarts daily; the owner re-authenticates weekly.
- No separate worker process for now.

**Rate limits** (owned by the IBKR adapter)
- Market-data lines:
  - default budget 70 at the open and 80 before the close, both configurable;
  - shrink on error 101;
  - every lease is released in `finally`.
- Outbound messages are capped at 40 per second, cancels included.
- Historical data has a separate pacer: at most 55 requests per 10 minutes, and no identical request within 15 s.
- 2026-10-16 (owner decision of the same day), after the main gateway stopped answering during the US chain refresh. Each gateway connection has one pacer for its non-historical requests (contract details, market-data snapshots, option parameters, account requests), shared by every caller of the process. The limits are ceilings, to use IBKR's capacity as fully as is safe: at most 40 request starts in any 1 s, 80% of the default 50/s ("Pacing Behavior", TWS API docs); at most 50 snapshots in flight, half of the default 100 market data lines; at most 5 contract details in flight, since derivative contract requests are internally paced and an ambiguous option request is throttled ("Option Chains", TWS API docs). The contract-detail limit adapts: it starts at 2, rises by 1 after an answer while the mean of the last 10 answer times is under 1 s, and halves (to no less than 1) when that mean is over 3 s. A contract-detail request waits 20 s for its answer; a timeout drops the limit to 1 and no new contract-detail request starts for 30 s, since the gateway keeps working on a request it cannot cancel. After 2 consecutive timeouts the listed-contract refresh of the run stops sending: the names not yet asked are skipped as `gateway_slow`, nothing is retried, and the job ends `partial`. A start is allowed only when, at that moment, the rate, the place in flight and the pause all allow it; nothing is reserved ahead. Every run of a job that makes paced requests logs one timing summary (request count per kind, answer-time p50, p90 and max per kind in ms, timeouts, the contract-detail limit's minimum and maximum, wall time; counts and times only). With `serve --record-timing` it is also stored, one row per run in the table `request_timing` of the main store; off by default, so the default serve adds no writer. `option-trader status --timing` prints the last 20 stored. Streaming quote subscriptions are not paced by it.
- 2026-10-17 (review of the pacer; owner decision of the same day for the unresolved-request rule). Every change of the contract-detail limit empties the window of answer times; the limit rises only when the window holds 10 answers again, all from requests started at the current limit; the first answer after a timeout is no sample, so the limit rises again only after 10 further answers. No new contract-detail request starts while the oldest one in flight on the connection is older than 5 s, or while more than 2 timed-out contract-detail requests of the connection have had neither their end (`contractDetailsEnd`) nor a terminal error for their request id; such a late end or error takes the request off the count and is not handed to the caller, and a new connection starts at zero. The timing summary also gives, per kind, the queue time (asked to started) p50, p90 and max in ms. The ceilings are unchanged.
- 2026-10-17 (owner decision of the same day). The pacer's limits are split between the gateway and the connection. Per gateway, keyed by its `(host, port)` and shared by every connection of the process to it (today the scheduler's market-data session, the option page's kept session and a fallback session): the adaptive contract-detail in-flight limit with its window of answer times, the oldest-in-flight check, the count of unresolved timed-out requests, and the 30 s pause after a timeout. Per connection, as before: the 40 request starts in any 1 s and the 50 market-data snapshots in flight. Why: the overload of 2026-10-16 happened inside one gateway process, which works through the contract-detail requests of all its connections, so a limit per connection let several connections together exceed what the gateway could answer; IBKR's requests-per-second rule ("Pacing Behavior", TWS API docs) is per client connection, so that window stays per connection. A connection that has not yet named its gateway keeps its own state. The timing summary still counts the requests of its run; the limit it reports is the shared one. The numbers are unchanged.
- Contract definitions are cached in DuckDB and refreshed daily. Only selected contracts are qualified.
- 2026-10-17 (owner decision of 2026-10-16). The listed-contract refresh no longer asks `reqContractDetails` once per expiry with the strike and the right left open: IBKR throttles such ambiguous option requests inside the gateway ("Option Chains", TWS API docs), and asking all 148 eligible expiries again every day overloaded the main gateway on 2026-10-16, although a contract id never changes. The chain itself comes from `reqSecDefOptParams`. For each active stock and each expiry of its chain definition in the plan window (`dte_min` to `dte_max` plus 3 days), the refresh takes the plan's strike range (`k` and the stored IV30, or `fallback_band`; `spread_depth` for a name on the spread list) around the stock's stored spot (its newest stored quote), widens it by `0.10 × spot` on each side for moves before the slots, and asks every chain strike inside it, integer or not, with both rights. A stock without a stored spot is skipped as `no_spot`. A contract already stored is never asked again; a contract answered with nothing is stored as not listed and not asked again for 1 day. Each contract is asked with one fully specified request (symbol, expiry, strike, right, exchange `SMART` for US or the chain definition's exchange for JP, currency, multiplier, trading class), through the connection's pacer, in groups of 30 whose answers are stored before the next group starts; exactly one matching answer stores the contract. The earlier stop after 2 consecutive timeouts stays. An expiry whose planned contracts are all known or not listed has its time moved, so the slot, which reads contracts stored within 24 hours, keeps it. Contracts and not-listed marks past their expiry are removed at the start of each refresh, instead of being kept 90 days. A slot or the option page that needs a contract not stored asks that one contract the same way. No request for an option leaves the strike or the right open. Design review of 2026-10-17 (same issue): an answer is exactly one result matching the whole request (expiry, strike, right, currency, exchange, trading class, multiplier), stored; or an empty end, or error 200 saying no security definition, stored as not listed with the code and text; or anything else (error 200 for an ambiguous request, several results, a differing result, a connectivity error, a timeout), which caches nothing. The run's stop flag is checked right before each send, after the pacer wait, and no answer arriving after the stop is used. The strike range is computed by the planner's own code (`plan.qualify_strikes`, which calls `_sides`). A contract is stored under its series (currency, exchange, trading class, multiplier) with one canonical strike text; the contracts of a version 1 store keep an empty series and count as known. A contract is removed once its expiry date has ended in the market's own time zone (US Eastern, Japan time). For JP the exchange, trading class, multiplier and expiry string are the chain definition's, never `SMART`; the currency is the stock's, since a chain definition carries none. The run logs its planned count before the first send, and its counts reconcile: planned = known + not listed cached + asked + unsent, asked = found + not listed + ambiguous + errors + timeouts.
- 2026-10-17 (owner decision of the same day: keeping the answers a few days longer does little harm). The first fully specified refresh, US, 2026-10-17, asked 8,170 contracts and all came back not listed, because a chain definition's strike set is the union over all expiries; the control job, run the same day, re-asked 50 known listed contracts through the same path and all 50 matched with the same contract id, so the negatives are real. Asking them again every day cost about 23 minutes of the main gateway in a job with a 30-minute deadline. So a not-listed answer is now kept 7 days plus a jitter drawn per entry, uniform in −2 to +2 days, and stored with the entry as its retry time; retention runs from the last answer and reading an entry never extends it (entries of an older store get a drawn retry time on upgrade). The refresh asks a cached pair before its time when (a) the underlying's chain definition gained the pair's strike since the entry was written (the refresh notes the chain's strikes with the time each was gained), (b) the stored spot moved by 5% or more from the spot stored with the entry, or (c) the contract was seen listed since by another path: a resolve that found it, a position or a stored quote; the entry is then removed at once. A slot or the option page that needs one contract trusts a not-listed entry younger than 1 day and asks that one contract again when the entry is older. The refresh's summary gives the cached negatives skipped, re-asked by each trigger and re-asked because they expired.

**Collection**
- Quotes are short streaming subscriptions, not snapshots. Each one is released early once the wanted fields arrive.
- Work is grouped by (underlying, expiry). Spot is sampled at the start and end of each group, and a group that spans too long or sees spot move too much is flagged incoherent. Raw rows are kept either way.
- The plan is written to the store before any request. Every planned item ends with exactly one final status, for example ok, partial, no data, no subscription, deadline, deferred or disconnected. Nothing is silently dropped.
- One year of daily IV and price history is backfilled for every tier.

**Initial scope**
- The scope is narrower than the earlier ±30% band. It is revised after the live measurement.
- The strike band is volatility-scaled: spot × exp(± k·σ·√T), where σ is the latest 30-day IV from history and k is configurable. ±30% is the fallback when σ is unknown.

**Order of work**
- Measure first:
  1. the guarded connection;
  2. a manual measurement script, run by the owner against IB Gateway, that is never part of CI;
  3. one name end to end.
- Then generalise.

## Consequences

- More code than a thin wrapper around `IB`, but no hidden account requests and no order path.
- IB Gateway has no manual windows competing for market-data lines. The budget leaves headroom, and error 101 shrinks it.
- Throughput numbers are assumptions until measured.

**2026-10-06 — Snapshot plan v2.** Owner decisions of 2026-10-05, after the first real US run planned 44,188 option items ordered by instrument and left 33 names without an option quote: the slot plans puts only (the right is a parameter; covered calls come later with their own rules); expiries with 21 to 45 DTE, counted in calendar days from the market's session date; listed strikes from spot down to `spot · exp(−k·σ·√T)` with `k = 1.5` and `T = DTE / 365`, or down to `spot · (1 − 0.30)` when σ is unknown, and the single highest listed strike at or below spot when none is in that range; names without an approved price are collected like every other name. There is no per-name cap: names take turns, each name's items ordered by strike nearest to spot first, then by the expiry nearest to 30 days, and all option items by (turn, instrument). `max_items` (3000) is a safety ceiling on option items only; items past it are stored as deferred, and an underlying is never deferred. Tier B is planned like tier A. The parameters live in `snapshots/params/plan_v2.toml` and the report names their hash. Mid-term and long-dated expiries and calls are no longer collected by the slot; the daily volatility grid (later issue) covers the term structure.

**2026-10-06 — Listed option contracts.** Chain definitions say which expiries and strikes may exist; listed contracts say which do. A read-only measurement on 2026-10-05 found that about 36% of the planned puts do not exist, and that asking IBKR which contracts exist takes about 5 s per (underlying, expiry) and is serialised: 33 names × 3 expiries is about 8 minutes, and 87 of 99 lookups would wait longer than the 60 s resolution limit inside a slot. So the listed contracts are fetched per (underlying, expiry) in the chain refresh job, for the plan window (21 to 45 DTE) plus a margin of 3 days, and stored with their fetch time. The slot plans only strikes that have a stored contract for every planned right, and streams them by the stored `con_id` without asking IBKR again. Listed contracts are kept 90 days after expiry, like observations.

**2026-10-07 — Snapshot plan v3.** Owner decision of 2026-10-07: start storing call quotes of 21 to 45 DTE now, because their history cannot be rebuilt later (covered calls are the third strategy). The groups, their order, the rotation and the stored fields below are the advisor's proposal after a second opinion of two models, not an owner decision.

- **Groups.** Per name and expiry in the window, with spot, σ and the window as in plan v2, a strike counts only when both rights are listed for it. `below` is the highest listed strike at or below spot, `above` the lowest listed strike above it; either may not exist.
  - `around_spot`: the put and the call at `below`, then the put and the call at `above`.
  - `puts`: puts at the listed strikes from spot down to `low = spot · exp(−k·σ·√T)`, or `spot · (1 − 0.30)` when σ is unknown, without `below`.
  - `calls`: calls at the listed strikes above spot up to `high = spot · exp(+k·σ·√T)`, or `spot · (1 + 0.30)` when σ is unknown, without `above`.
  - Both bounds are inclusive, with `k = 1.5` and `T = DTE / 365`. `min_strike_spacing` thins each side walking away from spot; the `around_spot` strike of the side counts as the first kept strike.
- **Rotation.** The names that got at least one option item, sorted by instrument, are rotated left by `(today's proleptic Gregorian ordinal × 2 + (1 for the close slot, else 0)) mod` their number; a name's index in the rotated list is its place. Underlying items stay in instrument order.
- **Order.** All `around_spot` items, then all `puts`, then all `calls`. `around_spot` is ordered by (expiry rank, place), expiry rank being plan v2's `(|DTE − 30|, DTE)`; the items of one name and expiry stay together. In `puts` and in `calls` a name's items are sorted by (strike rank walking away from spot, expiry rank) and numbered as turns, and all are ordered by (turn, place).
- **Ceilings.** `max_items` is one ceiling per group: `around_spot` 1000, `puts` 3000, `calls` 3000. The items of a group past its ceiling are stored as deferred; a group reaching its ceiling does not change the others, and an underlying is never deferred.
- **Stored.** `snapshot_plan_run` gains `plan_version` (3) and `param_hash` (sha256 hex of the parameter file), set on both runs of a slot; `snapshot_plan_item` gains `plan_group` (NULL for underlying items). A deferred item has the reason `max_items:around_spot`, `max_items:puts` or `max_items:calls`. Rows stored before this record keep NULL in the three columns and the reason `line_budget`.
- **Use.** The calls and the `around_spot` pairs are stored for later use; no rule reads them yet. The report follows two rules. Candidate section: a put is a candidate input only when the same job's underlying quote gives a usable spot and the put's strike is at or below it; a put without usable spot and the put at `above` are neither inputs nor in the deferred count nor in the number of planned puts, and no call is a candidate input. Snapshot section: it reports what the slot collected, so it counts every option item of the slot — calls and the `above` puts included — in the per-name cell, the problems column and the note, and the note's option part ends with the number planned per group, ` (现价两侧 A, put B, call C)`; a run stored before plan v3 keeps the earlier note.
- **Measurement.** On 2026-10-07 a US run of about 1,820 puts closed in about 167 seconds; the job deadline is 20 to 25 minutes. The listed-contract store already holds a call for every put (the per-expiry refresh fetches both rights), so no new contract lookup is needed.

The parameters live in `snapshots/params/plan_v3.toml`, the only version the program loads; `plan_v2.toml` stays in the repository unchanged. `rights` is no longer a parameter. Deadlines, line budgets, the collector and the observation rows are unchanged.

**2026-10-08 — Snapshot plan v4: deep puts for the spread list.** Owner decision of 2026-10-08: put credit spreads are offered only on names the owner lists, in two kinds. The bought leg of a spread needs a stored quote, and on a stored scan the lowest collected put lay between 7 % and 27 % below spot (median 16 %), often not low enough. Plan version 4 adds the parameter `spread_depth` (`0.15`, a fraction of spot, `0 < spread_depth < 1`): for a US name on the owner's spread list the lower bound of the `puts` group is `low − spread_depth × spot`, with `low` the bound of plan v3 (σ known or the fallback band). Nothing else changes: the `calls` and `around_spot` groups, the thinning, the order and the ceilings are those of plan v3, a name not on the list is planned exactly as before, and the extra strikes (below `low`) get the last turns of their name: they are ordered among themselves by the same (strike rank, expiry rank) rule after all its other puts of every expiry, so each other put keeps the turn it has without deep puts and no extra strike displaces one under the `puts` ceiling. JP names are never deep. The spread list is local owner configuration like the limits file (`~/.config/option-trader/spreads.toml`, versioned, hashed, never in the repository); a missing file means no spreads, and an invalid file fails the US snapshot run instead of being read as empty. A listed symbol that is not an active US watchlist stock is collected by nobody and named in the report's plan note; it does not change the run status. The parameters live in `snapshots/params/plan_v4.toml`, the only version the program loads; `plan_v3.toml` stays in the repository unchanged, and runs stored with plan version 3 still read back. No spread is computed, sized or shown yet.
