## Decisions

1. Scope. Part A is the Need type, two declarations, a containment test and the two keys; no behaviour of a stock capture changes. Part B is the index capture and one coverage line. The iv and delta of index rows are NOT computed here: they are a compute step beside the replay step, from the forward of each expiry by put-call parity and Black's formula on it (European exercise, so no dividend estimate and no early-exercise premium); that is why the selection is a set of call-put pairs around the money (Decision 7). Nothing to buy and nothing for the owner to decide: no subscription is needed.
2. The Need. `jobs.Need` (Interface) says what one strategy needs of the stored chains: the underlying group (today a watch group as `watch:<group>`; an index group is added with the first strategy that needs one), the rights, the DTE window, the strike reach as two multiples of a reference (`buy_price` or `spot`), the expiry kind (`all` or `monthly`) and the trading class. It is a declaration in the strategy's own manifest: `option_candidates/job.toml` gets two `[[need]]` rows, short put and covered call, and `optioncandidates.Needs()` returns them with the DTE window read from the `[params]` already there (one copy of 21 and 45). Every number of a declaration is trial unless a source is named in the comment of its row. The short put: rights P, reference `buy_price`, 0.80 to 1.05, all expiries; the top is the rule `strike - bid <= BuyPrice` read with a bid up to 5% of the buy price, the bottom the capture's own floor (the rule has none). The covered call: rights C, reference `spot`, 1.00 to 1.05, all expiries; the rule floors the strike at the sale price or cost (Decision 11 of `option-candidates.md`), the spot reach is the capture's own band and trial. Both are trial.
3. Containment. `optioneod.Covers(need)` answers nil when the stock capture takes everything the need declares, else an error naming the strategy and the dimension (group, rights, DTE, reach, expiry kind). It reads the embedded `job.toml` through `parseManifest`; reach by `buy_price` is compared with the keys of Decision 4, reach by `spot` with 1 - `strike_band_below` and 1 + `strike_band_above`. The reach is a bound of the band, not of the strikes taken: the capture takes at most `max_strikes` of them (Doubt 7). A new test in the root package `internal/jobs` (`need_test.go`) runs `Needs()` into `Covers` and fails on any error, and a second one shows a need that is outside is refused. It stays in the root package because that package already holds the repository-wide test of `api.go`, and the lint excludes test files from the import rule; the packages themselves import no sibling (Doubt 8). The index capture is covered by no `Need` yet: the first index strategy declares one and `Covers` learns the `index:` group then (Left out). The capture bounds computed as the union of the needs are a later step.
4. The buy-price range keys. `job.toml` and `job_jp.toml` gain `buy_low = "0.80"` and `buy_high = "1.05"`, decimal text, exact (`math/big`) as `rangeStrikes` is today; `rangeStrikes` takes them as arguments and `selectContracts` passes the manifest's. Both or neither: a manifest with one is invalid; with neither, an entry with a buy price keeps the band only. The plan hash of the manifest covers them (`canonical`) when set, so the two pinned hashes change once and the two pin tests are edited with the new values; the worker takes the values from the failing test (`## Checked by CI`).
5. One index job. `option_eod_index`: ledger dataset `obs.option_eod_index`, keys SPX, XSP and RUT in that order (the last key is the one a spent budget cuts), the slot of the stock run (XNYS, open + 90 minutes, run bound 16), budget 8 minutes, chunk 50, a job of its own so that an index failure never costs the stock day (the budget of the stock run is spent per run). This replaces the "two manifests" of the outline by one manifest of three index rows: each job id needs a row of the console table, a probe case in `preflight.go`, a family document, a family and exact job-list tests (Doubt 1).
6. Classes and expiries. SPX is class `SPX` only, the AM monthly (not `SPXW`, not `SPXO`); RUT is class `RUT` only (the monthly; `RUTW` is the weeklies, `MRUT` is left out); for both the expiries are the ones the class lists, within 0 to 60 days, the first two (`expiry_kind` `listed`). XSP is its one class `XSP`, filtered to the monthly: the third Friday of the month, or the Thursday before it when that Friday is not listed (a market holiday, as 19 June 2026). The expiry is keyed as the broker reports it, never moved to the Friday: the contract carries `20261217`, the instrument name and the `osi` identifier follow it. Weeklies, 0DTE and the PM classes are out (nothing in the plan needs them and they multiply the cost).
7. Selection of an index chain. The row of the class names three things: `sigma_index`, `spot_index` and `spot_divisor` (SPX: SPX, SPX, 1; XSP: SPX, SPX, 10; RUT: RUT, RUT, 1). Spot is the snapshot of the index (Last, else Close, else UndPrice, as `price` does today); when the snapshot gives nothing, which is what an unsubscribed index may do under data type 2, the spot is the last stored `trades` close of `spot_index` divided by `spot_divisor` (so XSP is the SPX close over 10, about 759 and not 7590) and the key still runs; with no close reader (Decision 10) there is no such spot and the key ends Retryable `no price`, as today. Sigma is the annualised (square root of 252) standard deviation of the last `sigma_days` = 20 daily log returns of the stored `trades` closes of `sigma_index`, read 70 calendar days back; with no reader, fewer than 21 closes or a reader error, `sigma_fallback` = 0.20, so the job always has a band. For each chosen expiry the half-width of the band is W = spot x `band_k` x sigma x sqrt(T), T the days to expiry over 365 with a floor of one day, `band_k` = 1.8. The strikes are spread over the band, not taken nearest to spot: N = `max_contracts` / (2 x the chosen expiries) strikes per expiry (80 and two expiries give 20). The pair nearest to spot among the multiples of the class's `strike_multiple` (SPX 50, XSP 5, RUT 5; the probes show steps of 5 and 1 near the money for SPX and XSP and 5 for RUT, the multiple thins the rest) is always taken (a tie keeps the lower strike) and is the at-the-money pair. The other N - 1 targets are spaced evenly from spot - W to spot + W; each takes the nearest listed strike that is a multiple and not yet taken (a tie keeps the lower), and a band with fewer than N such strikes takes all of them. Every strike is taken with both rights; after `listed` a strike of which one right is missing from the resolved contracts (the call lists, the put does not, or the reverse; the chain's strikes are a union over expiries, so it happens) is dropped, and so the stored rows are listed pairs. A listed pair is not a quoted pair: a quote with no bid and no ask is still stored (status other than 1) and the compute step uses only strikes with both mids. The strike text and the expiry text are the chain's own. Worked at 34 days and sigma 15%: W is 7591.86 x 1.8 x 0.15 x sqrt(34/365) = about 625 points, 8.2% (the 0.05-delta wing of a condor, about 7.5%, is inside it); 20 targets over 1250 points are 65 points apart, so on SPX they land on strikes 50 or 100 apart and cover the band; the stored contracts are 80 a class and a day.
8. What is stored. The rows of `mapRow` unchanged: bid, ask, sizes, volume, open interest where it comes, `iv_decimal` and `delta` NULL because the gateway sent none (status 1 when a bid or an ask is there), `market_data_type` of the quote. `underlying_price` is the index value of Decision 7: delayed by 15 minutes when it came from the snapshot, the last stored close (divided for XSP) when it came from the bar; the row does not say which (the dataset has no column for it; the job document says it). The reason rows record the missing iv and delta per field. The coverage line counts contracts, not complete pairs (Left out).
9. The class table. The rows `[[index]]` of `job_index.toml` are the table: `symbol`, `trading_class`, `strike_multiple`, `max_contracts`, `expiry_kind` (`listed` or `third_friday`), `sigma_index`, `spot_index`, `spot_divisor`. The exercise style and the settlement (AM or PM) of a class are not stored and not put in a table here: no code reads them in this step, `contract.proto` is owner-only and has no field for them, and the terms of every option are stored as `unknown` (`resolve.go:34`). They arrive with the compute step that needs them.
10. Closes behind an interface, and optional. The stored closes come through `optioneod.IndexCloseReader` (Interface), implemented in `cmd/ot` over the price bar reader and the index id reader that `compute_figures` already holds. The reader is optional: the job is built without it (a nil reader) and then uses `sigma_fallback` and no stored-close spot, so the capture does not depend on the bar wiring, which is built only while the bar database is configured and the broker was up at start; the closes are a refinement of the band and a fallback of the spot, never a condition of the day (adversarial point 2). Own implementation, because the stored `market.rv20` reading is written by `compute_figures` after the close behind a readings store this job does not open, and twenty log returns are a dozen lines; it sits behind the interface so a library or the compute core can replace it.
11. Wiring. A family `option_eod_index`, order 75 (after `compute_figures`, 70), built wherever `option_eod` is built (same broker, instrument store, option writer and clock), with the close reader when the price bar reader and the index id reader both exist and with none otherwise (one Warn line `option_eod_index runs without closes` names the case); a probe case in `preflight.go` as `option_eod` has, so that a scheduler started with the broker down brings it in when the broker is back; a row of the console table (today only, force refused with the reason `moment_mix`); the family document `docs/contracts/ot/jobs/option_eod_index.md`. // Need is what one strategy needs of the stored option chains (docs/design/option-capture-need.md). Group names
// the underlyings: a watch.toml group as watch:<group>. Ref is what Low and High multiply, buy_price or spot, and
// the reach is the strike range Low x Ref to High x Ref, both decimal text. Expiry is all or monthly. Every number

	Strategy       string
	Group          string
	Rights         []string

	Low, High      string
	Expiry         string
	TradingClass   string
}

## Doubts

1. One job, not three. The outline said one manifest per trading class. The classes are rows of one manifest here: a job id costs a console row (and its tests), a probe case, a family document, a family and exact job-list tests, three times. The price is that a Retryable error of the SPX key (budget spent, no price) ends the run and the retry runs all keys again (rows append, `option-chain-daily.md` Decision 7). If the owner wants separate jobs, Decision 5 and entries 3 and 4 change; the selection does not.
2. The index value under the production data type. The probes asked types 1 and 3, not 2 (the default of the gateway). If type 2 answers 354 for an unsubscribed index, the snapshot gives no price and the stored last close is used (Decision 7), so the day is still captured, centred on the last close; without a reader the key is Retryable. The first day shows which: `underlying_price` of the rows against the index value that day. The owner can set the data type of the gateway entry; this design does not. A stale close after a weekend can be off by several percent; the spread over the band (Decision 7) is wide enough to hold the money in most such cases, but the pair at the money is the one nearest the used spot.
3. The cost is an estimate. About 240 contracts: 240 serial resolves (about 1 second each in the probes, the details lane), then 5 chunks of 50 that each end at the 20 second item timeout because no model field arrives (`spec.py`), so roughly 5 to 7 minutes against the budget of 8; that is tight, and RUT, the last key, is the one a spent budget cuts (the budget is checked before every broker call and a spent budget keeps the rows written). If a chunk of index options answered slower, `max_contracts` is the key to lower. The run shares its slot and its pyside lanes with `option_eod`; whether the scheduler starts the two runs at once or in turn was not read. Not measured through the chunk path (Left out).
4. `compute_figures` will list SPX, XSP and RUT as underlyings of the day (`subjects.go:41-47` has no type filter), as it lists N225. Its iv step reads NULL iv as 0, finds no usable expiry and stores a `missing_input` reading per rule and day, and the run does not fail (review r1, answer 2: read in `iv30.go` and `steps.go`, not run). A filter on the type would be a change of that job. The first day shows it.
5. The `osi` identifier of an SPX or RUT monthly follows the broker's expiry (261217) while the OCC symbol follows the Friday (261218). Only the classes SPX, XSP and RUT are stored and their symbols differ, so no two instruments collide; any later join of these rows to an outside source must key by class, strike, right and the broker's expiry, not by the `osi` text. The identifier is part of the instrument row, so correcting it later means a new instrument per contract.
6. The plan hash of `option_eod` and `option_eod_jp` changes once at deploy (the two keys). The deploy that carries it may re-plan the day in progress; the hash changed before for the window keys.
7. The owner window (`NewWithWatchWindow`) can narrow the DTE and strikes of the stock capture at run time below a declared need, and `max_strikes` 24 caps the strikes taken inside the reach; the containment test reads the embedded manifest only.
8. `internal/jobs/need_test.go` imports both job packages. It is a test file, which the lint excludes, and the packages import no sibling in their own code (entry 2 item 6), but the jobs row says "importing another jobs package" is forbidden; the file stays in `internal/jobs` as decided, and a move to `cmd/ot` would put it in the ot row and change entry 2's Files.
9. XSP and RUT spreads at the open are unknown: the probes ran before the open and saw spreads of 0.11 to 0.20 on 12 to 15 dollar XSP options and 1.1 to 1.2 on 60 to 70 dollar RUT options. If rows are mostly one-sided, the class stays in step 0 as stored data and no more.
10. The con_ids of SPX (416904), XSP (137851301) and RUT (416888) are the probes'; `bars_refresh` keeps `con_id = 0` for SPX and resolves it, so the instrument that the option rows hang on and the one the closes belong to are the same row only if both resolve through `ibkr_conid`. A difference changes nothing in step 0; the reader finds the closes by name. The MIC of the RUT row is copied from the SPX row and not read by the selection.
11. Every number is trial: `band_k` 1.8 (the 5% tail of a normal is 1.645 sigma; arithmetic), the multiples 50, 5 and 5, the cap 80 a class (240 in all), `sigma_days` 20, `sigma_fallback` 0.20, the first two monthlies as the DTE reach (the first may be under 30 days and the second over 60 is out, so a rule needing 30 to 45 days may find one expiry), and the needs' reach. None is backtested; the replay settles them.
