## Goal

On `/job-console` of `ot serve --owner-edit` the page lists every job. Sixteen jobs can be run from the page: four for any day up to today, because their `Run` uses the day it is given, and twelve for today only, because their `Run` answers for the day of now or guards for it; for today the console starts a key only from the job's own slot on, as `Reconcile` does, and before it the page shows "runs at HH:MM UTC". For each of the other eight the page says why it cannot be run and what would make it runnable. For a runnable job the owner chooses a day (fixed to today in the job's own zone for a today-only job) and force yes or no, presses `Run`, and the page shows the request as `queued` (with the reason when it is held), `running`, then `done`, `refused`, `failed`, `interrupted` or `withdrawn` (each with a stored reason code), with one line per key: what that run produced, whether the key is present now, and a reason code. A queued request can be withdrawn from the page. After a reload the last requests and their per-key lines are still there. Force is offered for five jobs only: `account_capture`, `quote_snapshot`, `option_eod`, `option_eod_jp` and `calendar_refresh`, where a second run of a present key is read in the code to give the same rows or to replace them; for the other eleven the page offers "run missing" and says why. A key that is missing or `no_value` gets the ordinary run and mark that `ot backfill` gives; a key that is already present and forced is run again bare, and the line says what it returned without a mark or a run row. The run is done by the scheduler process, which holds the service env and the run lease, as one turn of the ticker's own worker; `ot serve` only writes request rows and reads results. No terminal step.

Idempotent means, here and in `## Idempotency of the jobs`: a second run of a job for a day adds no duplicate row; a row is changed only by the job's own revision or upsert rule on changed content. Three jobs key a row by an attempt counter, so a forced second run adds one row under the next attempt: that is the job's own key, not a duplicate of the same attempt. The table names the rule of each job and says which lines were read; the page does not claim more (Doubt 6).

## Decisions

1. **The page writes a request; the scheduler's worker runs it.** `ot serve` inserts one row into `settings.job_request` (job id, day, force). The scheduler process takes a waiting request as one turn of the ticker's worker (Decision 8), runs `BackfillWith` for that day and the job's keys (`jobDatasets`, `cmd/ot/backfill.go:124-165`, resolves a job to its datasets), and stores the result in the same row. Why not `serve` shelling out to `ot backfill`: `serve` would need the runner DSN, the vendor keys and a gateway, which it holds none of; a page that can start a process is a larger authority than a page that can write one row. Why not a goroutine beside the ticker (revision 2): a second consumer shares the lease with the ticker's Reconcile, outlives the ticker's drain and needs its own yield, bound, cap and expiry rules; the worker is already serial and awaited at a stop.
2. **Two tables, a unique index that holds force.** `settings.job_request`: `id` (identity), `job_id`, `session_day`, `force`, `requested_at` (default `now()`), and the columns of the scheduler: `state` (`queued` default, `running`, `done`, `refused`, `failed`, `interrupted`, `withdrawn`), `started_at`, `finished_at`, `reason` (a code), `hold_job` and `hold_at` (why a queued request waits, Decision 8), `keys jsonb` (the object `{"v":1,"lines":[...]}`, one line per key with the fields of `KeyRun`: `dataset`, `subject`, `group`, `status`, `error_code`, `reason_code`, `returned`, `usable`, `attempt`, `mark`, `due_at`). A partial unique index `(job_id, session_day, force) WHERE state IN ('queued','running')` makes a double press of the same kind one request (the insert is `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING RETURNING`; with no row returned the open row is read, and when that read finds none because the request finished between the two, the insert is tried once more and then `Enqueue` answers `EnqueueBusy` and the post 503) and lets a press of the other force value make its own request; both run, one after the other. There is no 409 for a force mismatch and no silent drop of the owner's force; the one 409 is a press while that open request is being withdrawn (Decision 11). `settings.job_request_withdraw` (`request_id` primary key referencing the request, `withdrawn_at` default `now()`) holds the withdrawals: the web role can insert there and cannot update a request. There is no cap on open requests and no floor on the day (revision 2 had both): the owner is one person, and each request is one serial turn. No role can delete a row; the page lists the newest 20 and the tables have no retention (Doubt 9). The page reads the view `rd.job_request_r1` (every column named, with `withdrawn_at` joined in) with `OT_PG_DSN_PAGES`.
3. **Who may act, said plainly.** Today the page token of `/owner-settings` lets whoever holds it change the owner's rules and names. After this design the same token also lets him queue a run of the 16 runnable jobs (four for any day not after today, twelve for today from the slot on), which spends vendor quota (Alpha Vantage 25 a day for the fundamentals source), broker pacing and the worker's time, and with force appends rows; it can withdraw a queued request and can never delete or edit a row (the web role cannot). The token binds no route (`Check` has no route argument, `settings.go:243-260`), so one token works for the three posts for 15 minutes. Reach is local: the remote address must be loopback, the Host a loopback name with the listen port, a cross-origin post is refused, and `serve` refuses `--owner-edit` on another address (`serve.go:52-54`). The web role is the existing `ot_w_settings` and the line `OT_PG_DSN_SETTINGS`, with `SELECT` and a column-level `INSERT` (of the columns the post sets) on the two new tables in a schema it already holds. Schema `settings` is insert-only for every role but the owner today; this design makes `ot_runner` the first non-owner role to hold `USAGE` on it and an `UPDATE` in it: on seven named columns of `job_request` (`state`, `started_at`, `finished_at`, `reason`, `keys`, `hold_job`, `hold_at`) and nothing else, with `SELECT` on both tables and no grant in `ops`. `0033_underlying.sql` uses a narrow role of its own for a similar need; roles `ot_w_jobrequest` and `ot_w_jobrequest_run` would narrow the two sides at the cost of a change of `internal/store/setup`, an `.env` line and an owner step each (Doubt 4). No new role, no new `.env` line, no `ot setup pg` change here. The owner's merge of this design is his word for the widened authority.
4. **A forced run of a present key is a bare run, a run of any other key is the ordinary run, and the console has no tag of its own.** `ForceBackfiller.BackfillWith(ctx, day, datasets, BackfillOptions{Force, Job, Hold})` takes the lease as `Backfill` does and goes over the selected keys in order. For each key, in this order:
   - `present` and no force: group `already_present`.
   - not due (Decision 7): not run, group `not_due`, with `DueAt`.
   - `present` and force: a **bare run**, group `rerun`: `job.Run(ctx, day, []ExpectedKey{k})` under the lease, with no run row, no mark and no heartbeat of its own; force is offered only for the jobs of the force column (Decision 6). The line takes the returned and usable counts and the kind of the job's error (never its text, `safeErrText` is for logs); an error of kind `NoValue` is stored as the reason `no_value` with no error code, as `RunKey` treats it, and reads `nothing_found`. A bare run writes nothing that the scheduler, the gap syncer, `ops.Status` or `run-once` read; what the job keeps in its own memory or stores by its own rule is the job's (Doubt 16).
   - missing or `no_value` with a live lease on one of its runs: group `lease_held`.
   - missing or `no_value` otherwise: the ordinary run of `Backfill` (`backfillKey` through `RunKey`, reason `backfill`, the closeAt of the key's window or zero), with the ordinary verdict and mark, group `ran`, each key through `RunKey` itself under the deadline now plus the job's `RunBound` (not `deadlineFor`, which for a key inside its window lies hours away), so every key has its own bound (Decision 8). Unlike `Backfill` it runs a key whose window is still open, because the key is no longer held (Decision 7): that is the attempt `Reconcile` makes on its next tick, with the same closeAt, so an empty answer before the close is `empty_before_close` and is retried, and a counted run counts as a scheduler attempt would: a counted `done` run ends the scheduler's attempts for the key and a `partial` one allows one more (`nextAttempt`, `reconcile.go:361`).
   - before each key after the first the context and `opt.Hold` are checked (Decision 8); the keys not reached get group `not_reached` and no run row.
   **Readers of run rows (revisions 3 to 5 had a reason `console` with six readers, `withoutConsole`, a guard of `closeRow` and a skip in `lastRuns` and `ops.Status`; none is needed).** A console press leaves no run row (bare run) or one ordinary `backfill` run row for a key that was missing or `no_value`, which is what `ot backfill` leaves and what a scheduler attempt looks like to every reader, because no code reads the reason `backfill`. The one exception is a key cut by a console stop (a deploy during a console turn, Decision 8): it gets the reason `console_stop`, which is no crash, is not counted and opens no gap. Three readers ignore it as they ignore `empty_before_close`: `nextAttempt` and `closeRow` (`reconcile.go:352,378`) and `gapSyncer.keyKind` (`gaps.go:166`). `dayState`, `lastRuns` and `ops.Status` need nothing: a `console_stop` row is an ordinary `abandoned` row to them.
   - `nextAttempt`, `expireIfClosed`, `closeRow`, `markCrashed`, `verdict`: every other run is an attempt like any other; no filter, so no empty-slice case for `closeRow` (`reconcile.go:388`: `expireIfClosed` returns early on `len(runs) == 0`).
   - `SyncKeys` after the request gives the gap kind that the same run would have given had `Reconcile` made it.
   - a bare run writes no row, so it can be neither counted nor hidden, and a crash during it leaves no trace but the request row (Doubt 15).
   Where this differs from `Reconcile`, on purpose: `Reconcile` skips any key that has any mark (`reconcile.go:255-257`) and holds unmarked keys only; the console, like `ot backfill`, also runs a `no_value` key and holds a forced present key before its slot, and it does not consult `nextAttempt`, so it can add a counted run to a key whose scheduler attempts had ended. The cost: a missing key that the owner runs is a real attempt, which may write `no_value`; that is the owner's ask ("run it") and the behaviour of `ot backfill`. A present key is never touched.
5. **A call selects keys by job, and a line says what that attempt did.** `BackfillOptions.Job` is a job id: with it set, `BackfillWith` keeps from the keys of the listed datasets only those whose job (`p.job[k].ID()`) is that id, so pressing `option_open_jp` never runs the keys of `option_open` that share its dataset. `BackfillResult` gains `Keys []KeyRun`: `Key`, `Group` (`already_present`, `not_due`, `rerun`, `ran`, `lease_held`, `not_reached`), `DueAt` for `not_due`, for `rerun` the `Returned`, `Usable`, `ErrorCode` (the kind of the job's error) and `ReasonCode` (`no_value` for a `NoValue` error) of the bare run, for `not_reached` the `ReasonCode` `slot_near` or `stopped`, for `ran` the `Status`, `ErrorCode`, `ReasonCode`, `Returned`, `Usable`, `Attempt` of the new run row, and `Mark`, the mark after the call (`present`, `no_value` or empty; read as `backfillKey` reads it, `backfill.go:295-311`). The outcome of a line comes from the group and those facts; `Mark` is shown beside it and is never the outcome. The six old lists stay and are filled as before for a call without options. The reason is always a code, never the text of a job error; what the job itself says for "nothing" (`after session`, `missed slot`) is in the log only (Left out).
6. **What can be run from the page is a table, and the page lists every job.** `cmd/ot` holds one table of the 24 job ids; each row declares a `kind` (one declared field per job; there is no `LiveDay` in the runner):
   - `any_day` (four): the `Run` uses the day; the post and the claim accept a day not after today of `marketZone`;
   - `today_only` (twelve): the row also declares the zone, `America/New_York`, or `Asia/Tokyo` for `option_eod_jp`; the post and the claim accept only the day that is today in that zone;
   - `listed_only` (eight): a reason code, a text and a sentence of what would make it runnable: `no_lower_time_bound` (`option_open`, `option_open_jp`: a run before its slot would store pre-open prices as the open; runnable when its `Run` has a lower bound at the slot instant), `no_day_guard` (the two plan jobs: a late run makes a key that the open job answers late; runnable when the plan job refuses the day and the time that the pull refuses), `heavy_and_shares_cap` (the three resolve jobs: the budget exceeds the run bound so a run is always cut, and the per-day cap in memory is shared with the scheduler; runnable when a console run has a budget and a cap of its own), `caches_failures` (`option_ledger`: a failed result is cached for the day, so a run neither retries nor can be forced; runnable when the cache keeps only successes or `Run` takes a force flag).
   Each runnable row also declares `force`. **Force is `yes` for five jobs**, where a second `Run` of a present key is read in the code to give the same rows or to replace them: `account_capture`, `quote_snapshot`, `option_eod` and `option_eod_jp` (a deterministic id, `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`) and `calendar_refresh` (an upsert). It is `no`, with a reason code and a text, for the other eleven: `attempt_rows` (`position_marks`, `earnings_refresh`, `dividend_capture`: a re-run adds a row under the next attempt), `epoch_and_caps` (`bars_refresh`: a restatement writes a new epoch and the first-pull cap is memory that the scheduler's runs share), `new_run_id` (`option_candidates`: changed inputs make a new run id and a second run of rows), `quota` (`fundamentals`: the Alpha Vantage quota is 25 a day), `revision_rows` (`fred_series`, `compute_figures`: a vendor revision or a changed state is a new row) and `not_proved` (`fx_refresh`, `iv_history`, `underlying_resolve`: nothing read proves a second run equal). For a force-no job the page offers only "run missing": no force switch, and the text says why; the post refuses `force` for it (422) and the host refuses it again at the claim (`refused` `not_runnable`).
   The page shows every job. A runnable job has the form (for a today-only job the day field holds today and cannot change; the force switch only for a force-yes job); a job with a `note` shows it beside the form (`quote_snapshot`: "its three keys, 09:45, 12:30 and 15:30 ET, share one scheduled instant, 15:30 ET, so all three wait for it; only the 15:30 key can produce, and only until ten minutes after it; a 09:45 or 12:30 key that is still missing ends no value (missed slot), as the scheduler's own tick at 15:30 would write"); the sentence of what would make a listed-only job runnable is the field `needs_text`; a listed-only job shows its reason and what would make it runnable, and no form. The post refuses a listed-only job, an id not in the table, and a day that the kind does not allow (422); the post knows no slot times, so before the slot the post is accepted and the request ends `refused` `not_due` with "runs at HH:MM UTC" (Decision 7). The host checks the day rule again when it claims the request, in the job's zone from the injected clock, and ends the request `refused` `not_today` (a request can wait across midnight) or `not_runnable`. A drift test asserts that the ids of the table are the jobs the wiring builds. `ot backfill` is unchanged: it already prints a whole-day refusal and the keys still missing and exits non-zero on either (`cmd/ot/backfill.go:114-122,168-176`, `refusalError` and `missingError`), and it still marks a slot-only key `no_value` for a past day as on main (Doubt 5).
7. **A console run holds a key exactly as `Reconcile` holds it.** `Backfill` lists a key inside its window as `InWindow` and does not run it, so "Run today" during the session would do nothing, and running every in-window key would be a free pass: the scheduler holds a `Scheduled` job's keys until its slot instant, because a row written earlier is kept (`DO NOTHING`) and marks the key present, so an early press of `account_capture`, `position_marks`, `bars_refresh`, `fx_refresh` or `iv_history`, or for today `compute_figures` or `option_candidates`, would freeze a stale row and the slot would never run it. So `BackfillWith` runs a key inside its window only if the key is not held (`backfillPlan` returns the `held` set and the sessions of `pDay`, which it computes today and drops; a job whose calendar has no session that day is not in `pDay` and is never held, as in `Reconcile`; `slotAt` gives `DueAt`, and `TestNotDueAgreesWithReconcileHeldSet` guards that the two agree): the held set of `backfillPlan`'s own pass over the sessions of the day (Decision 4, `notDue` factored into a helper `slotAt(job, sess)` that returns the slot instant from the job's `Schedule()`, which `BackfillWith` also uses for `DueAt`). A past day is never held, so any past day of an any-day job runs at once. For today, before the slot, a key is `not_due` and the page says "runs at HH:MM UTC"; a request whose lines are all `not_due` ends `refused` `not_due`. No table of times is added: the instant is the one the scheduler computes from the job's declared schedule. An answer that is empty before the close leaves the key unmarked and the scheduler's own ticks go on (the ordinary `empty_before_close` rule).
8. **A console request is one turn of the ticker's worker; it is held while a slot is near; the bound is the job's own.** `runner.ConsoleTurner` is an optional interface of the Reconciler, found by a type assertion like `Prober`: `ConsoleTurn(ctx, hold SlotHold) (took bool, err error)`, where `SlotHold` is `func(bound time.Duration) (jobID string, at time.Time, held bool)`. The ticker does the rest:
   - the loop submits a turn with `console` set only when it submits no reconcile turn: a due slot or tick is submitted before a waiting request, and a request waits at most until the next wake of the loop (at most 30 s, `ceiling`) when the worker is idle; one request per turn; the poll is one indexed `SELECT` on the refresh turn the loop submits anyway;
   - `hold(bound)` answers from the schedule the worker has published (`t.starts`): the first start of any enabled job in `(now, now+bound]` of the injected clock, or an earlier start the ticker has not yet marked covered (the loop decides `slotDue` at its own `now`, and the worker may spend up to the start phase before it asks), `held` true; a schedule not yet published answers `("", zero, true)`, and a schedule that failed to refresh keeps the last published one (`ticker.go:358-362`);
   - `ConsoleTurn` (entry 7) reads the waiting requests, oldest first, takes the first whose job is not held, with `bound` = `turnMax(job)`: the start phase (a context of 1 minute of the injected clock that `ConsoleTurn` puts on `Queued`, each `Hold` and `Claim`, so the minute is enforced) plus the job's `RunBound` for the first key (`runner.Bounded`; a job without it gets the default of 30 minutes, `defaultRunBound` in `runkey.go`) plus the 30-second timeout of `Finish` plus the 2-minute timeout of `SyncKeys` (`syncTimeout`, `ticker.go:43`). The `RunBound` of `quote_snapshot`, `option_eod` and `option_eod_jp` is 16 minutes, so their `turnMax` is 19.5 minutes; for the jobs of 5 to 10 minutes it is 8.5 to 13.5. A request it passes over gets `hold_job` and `hold_at` (the start of the slot) written on its row, so the page shows "starts after HH:MM UTC (the slot of <job>)" or "held: the schedule is not read yet"; a request that is taken clears them. There is no expiry: a held request waits as long as it must, and the owner can withdraw it;
   - **each key runs under its own job's `RunBound`**, from its own start, as a scheduler run does; there is no bound for the whole request. A request of many keys (`compute_figures`, `bars_refresh`) is not cut: before every key after the first `BackfillWith` asks `opt.Hold` (the `SlotHold` of the ticker) with that key's `RunBound` plus the `Finish` and `SyncKeys` timeouts, and when a slot start lies inside it stops there: the keys not reached get group `not_reached` with the reason `slot_near` and no run row, and the request ends `failed` `slot_near` ("stopped before the next key because the slot of <job> starts at HH:MM UTC; press Run again after it": keys already present are skipped, so the rest runs). No key is cut by the console for a slot, so no key gets a strike for it;
   - because a request starts only when no start of a slot lies within `turnMax`, every next key is held the same way and every phase ends within its limit, **a console turn never delays a slot**, for a job that honours its context (a job that does not holds the worker as a job in `Reconcile` does, Doubt 2). A tick (a Reconcile that retries keys, not a slot) that comes due during a turn finds the worker busy, is not marked covered and is retried every `busyRetry` until the whole request has ended (the hold before each key looks at slots only), so a request of many keys can delay a tick, never a slot (`ticker.go:236-247`); a tick's retries are never lost to a held lease, because the worker is never in two places;
   - **a stop or an executable change during a turn** (a withdrawal does not stop a running request): the worker gets `drainGrace`, then its context is cancelled (`ticker.go:116-148`). `BackfillWith` cancels the key in flight with the private cause `errConsoleStop` (a key context of its own that follows the context `BackfillWith` was called with, bridged by `context.AfterFunc` so that a lease renewal that fails and cancels the plain way is not tagged), so `RunKey` writes that key's row with the reason `console_stop` (status `abandoned`, or `partial` with rows), which is no crash strike (Decision 4); the keys not reached get no row; the turn ends `interrupted` with the reason `stopped` and the lines so far on the request row, and is not run again by itself. A key that reaches its own `RunBound` is cut as on main (`abandoned_stop`, a strike: the job's own bound, as for a scheduler run), and a job that ignores its context holds the worker as a job in `Reconcile` does (the two-hour `reconcileTimeout`, `ticker.go:41`); a process killed after `killGrace` leaves a lapsed `running` row that the reconciler counts as a crash, as it does for its own runs (Doubt 16).
9. **The turn follows one order.** In `ConsoleTurn`: read the waiting requests; for each that carries a withdrawal, `Finish` it `withdrawn`; take the first not held and `Claim` it (`UPDATE ... SET state='running' WHERE id=$1 AND state='queued'`, one row or none); resolve the job among the jobs of the reconciler now in use (the host keeps the job set and the `Backfiller` of the last `build`, so a job a probe brought back is there; a job in the table but not in that set ends `refused` `left_out`, an id not in the table or `listed_only` ends `refused` `not_runnable`); `jobDatasets` (a job with no dataset ends `refused` `no_key`); `BackfillWith` with `Job`, `Hold` and the request's `Force`. **The end state, in this precedence:** (1) if `Plan` or `BackfillWith` returned with the context done, `context.Cause(ctx)` decides: `Canceled` is `interrupted`, whatever error text came with it; (2) `Refused` set: `RefusedNoPlan` is `refused` `no_plan`, `RefusedNoKey` (a selected dataset has no key on the day, `selectKeys` in `backfill.go`; a holiday or a weekend of the job's calendar) is `refused` `no_key`; (3) `Retryable` from the lease (a `run-once` or `ot backfill` of the owner holds it) is `failed` `lease_held`; a `Plan` error is `failed` `plan_failed`; any other error is `failed` `error`; (4) a result with no line `ran` or `rerun` and at least one `not_due` is `refused` `not_due`; (5) a result with a key `not_reached` for `slot_near` is `failed` `slot_near`; (6) otherwise `done`. A request stopped by a cancel or by `slot_near` keeps the lines of the keys done and marks the others `not_reached`. **Every end state stores a reason:** `done` `completed`, `interrupted` `stopped`, `withdrawn` `withdrawn`, and for the others the codes of Decision 12. `Finish` is written with a context that is not cancelled and a 30-second timeout. After `Finish` of a `done` or `failed` request that has a line `ran`, `KeyGapSyncer.SyncKeys(day)` runs as `ot backfill` does (`cmd/ot/backfill.go:68`) under a 2-minute timeout; otherwise it does not run (a bare re-run changes no run row or mark, nothing ran, or the next Reconcile turn syncs an interrupted request's gaps). A withdrawal of a request that is `running` is ignored: it ends by itself; a withdrawal inserted after the queue read and before `Claim` is not seen either, so that request runs and the withdrawal row stays unused. **At start, the host's `Start` sets every `running` row `failed` with the code `restart`** (the fence makes this process the only consumer; the scheduler calls `Start` before the ticker runs, entry 9), so a row left by a kill, a frozen loop or a turn that missed the 5 s grace cannot hold the unique index for good. A request is never run again by itself. **Failure of the store.** `Start` is tried before the ticker runs and again at the start of every `ConsoleTurn` until it succeeds, so a database migrated after the scheduler started needs no restart; until it succeeds the turn is `took=false` and one warn line a minute is written. A `Queued` or `Claim` error is the same no-turn. A `Finish` error leaves the row `running`, which the next `Start` fails `restart`. The page's reader answering an error gives the gap `job_requests_unavailable` and no list, and the post answers 503 with a fixed text and no DSN; a request queued while the scheduler has no console shows the stale-queue sentence of Decision 10. A deploy ahead of the migration therefore degrades with a reason and breaks nothing; Rollout still holds entry 9 until the migration has run. A panic in a job is the panic a `Reconcile` would have: no `recover` is added, the process ends, the row stays `running` and the next start fails it `restart`.
10. **The job table is static, checked by a test, and the page makes no claim about the scheduler.** The scheduler does not publish a capability table (Left out), so the page cannot show beforehand that the scheduler holds a job out: the request ends `refused` `left_out`. A `queued` request shows the time it was queued and one sentence: its hold if it has one ("starts after HH:MM UTC (the slot of <job>)"); if it has a withdrawal, "withdrawal pending: it takes effect when the scheduler's worker is next free (a running reconcile can take up to two hours)"; if it is older than 2 minutes with neither, "still queued: the scheduler may have no console yet (it tries again on every turn), or its worker is busy"; otherwise "waits for the scheduler's worker; a running reconcile or console run goes first".
11. **A post, a withdraw and a reload.** `POST /owner-job-console` has the field `action` (`run` or `withdraw`) and the token. `run`: fields `job`, `day`, `force`; 422 with a fixed text and no row for an id that is not in the table, a `listed_only` job, `force` for a force-no job, a `day` that is not `YYYY-MM-DD`, a `today_only` job whose day is not today in the job's zone, or an `any_day` job whose day is after today of `marketZone` (`cmd/ot/day.go:13`); any earlier day is accepted for an `any_day` job (no floor); 409 with the fixed text "this request is being withdrawn; press Run again when it shows withdrawn" when `Enqueue` finds the open row of the same job, day and force with a withdrawal (`EnqueueWithdrawing`); 503 with a fixed text when the store errors or answers `EnqueueBusy`; 303 to `/job-console?request=<id>` otherwise, also for a press that finds the same request open (`EnqueueExisting`), so a reload never repeats the post. `withdraw`: field `id`; 422 for an id that is not a request, otherwise the insert into `job_request_withdraw` (idempotent) and 303; a request that is `running` ignores it. **Zones and plans.** The ticker plans by the UTC day (`dayOf`, `ticker.go:557`), a request carries its own day, and the day is checked against the job's zone. The only Tokyo job is `option_eod_jp`: before its freeze (15:50 JST) it writes nothing, and from 09:00 JST the Tokyo date and the UTC day are the same; between 00:00 and 09:00 JST the Tokyo date is one ahead of the UTC day, has no stored plan, and the request ends `refused` `no_plan` (Doubt 5). A day with no stored plan ends `refused` `no_plan` for every job: the page cannot create a plan and says so; a day on which the job's calendar has no session (a holiday or a weekend) ends `refused` `no_key` and the page says "not a session: the job plans no key on this day".
12. **The page.** `page.job_console.v1`: `schema_version`, `schema`, `generated_at`, `title`, `sentence`, `form` (null when the route is off, else `post_path`, `expires`, `mac`), `gaps` (including `job_requests_unavailable` and `no_such_request`), `jobs` (`id`, `kind`, `today` (the date of now in the job's zone), `note` (or null), `force` (true for a force-yes job), `force_why` (the text for a force-no job), `why_text` and `needs_text` for a listed-only job), `requests` (the newest 20: `id`, `job`, `day`, `force`, `state`, `state_text`, `at_text`, `reason`, `keys`), `selected` (the id of `?request=`, shown even when older than the 20; an unknown id gives the gap `no_such_request`) and `refresh_seconds` (5 while a shown request is `queued` or `running`, else 0). `at_text` is the instant in UTC (`2006-01-02 15:04 UTC`). ## Doubts (least sure)

1. A held request can wait for ever and a long-bound request can be passed over for a short one. The slots of the day are 15:30 ET (`quote_snapshot`), 16:00 (`option_eod` freezes at 16:15) and then every 5 to 15 minutes to 16:45 (the offsets of the manifests); with `turnMax` at 8.5 to 13.5 minutes for the jobs of 5 to 10 minutes and 19.5 minutes for `quote_snapshot`, `option_eod` and `option_eod_jp` (16-minute bound), a start lies inside it from about 15:10 ET to about 17:00 ET for those three and from about 15:47 for the others, so up to about 110 minutes of the day plus the Tokyo slots; a press of `option_eod` after its slot waits until about 17:00; and that is also when a post-close press of a today-only job is useful (it is not yet due before its slot, and held after it until the cluster ends); the request shows why, can be withdrawn, and has no expiry by choice. The default bound of a job without `Bounded` is 30 minutes.
2. A job that ignores its context holds the worker and the lease until it returns (same as `Reconcile`, `reconcileTimeout` two hours), and a turn that loses the 5 s kill race leaves the row `running` until the next start turns it `failed` `restart`; for that time the unique index blocks the same job, day and force. Every code merge ends a running turn as `interrupted` after 30 s, which with a few merges a day is common.
3. The poll rides the refresh turn of the loop, at most every 30 seconds; the page reloads every 5 seconds, so a request can wait up to 30 seconds behind an idle worker, and behind a reconcile turn of up to two hours. The page says "still queued: the scheduler may have no console yet" after 2 minutes and makes no other claim about the scheduler.
4. The token is also a settings and underlyings token for 15 minutes, and the reverse (`Check` binds no route); a holder can queue runs of 16 jobs, one turn each, with no cap, and spend vendor quota (Alpha Vantage 25 a day). The web role is `ot_w_settings` and the runner role `ot_runner` with a column-level `UPDATE`, the first non-owner update in schema `settings`; two narrower roles are cleaner (the 0033 precedent) and cost an owner step each.
