Goal. The seller needs the real contracts, with con_ids, of every watched US stock stored before the open, taken the same morning, without depending on the day before (rev 14: with `list_ttl_days` 7 a group listed in the last seven days stays fresh, `option-open-gap-jp.md` Decision 4; the same-morning guarantee is that a group with no fresh answer is listed that morning) (owner, 12:36Z: "必须有兜底方案"). Today the per-strike resolve guesses strikes and asks IBKR one by one. One `reqContractDetails` per (underlying, class, expiry) with no strike returns the real list, calls and puts together (`probes-5-6.txt`). This design adds that call and one new job that uses it, a pass at 06:15 ET that stores the lists for the open pull at 09:35 ET. It changes no existing job, no page and no plan; main's per-strike path keeps running beside it as the fallback. Design B consumes the stored lists. The indexes SPX, NDX and RUT are not in this design (`## Left out`).

## Decisions

1. **The rpc.** `ContractsService.ListContracts(underlying, expiry, right, trading_class) -> ListContractsResponse{ repeated ContractSpec }`: one `reqContractDetails` with no strike; an empty `right` means both rights in one answer (Decision 7); con_id, right, strike, multiplier, class per contract. Read only. A timeout, disconnect or gateway error is a gRPC error, never a partial list (IB returns the whole detail list or an error). `list_contracts` joins the log `WORDS`, and `ListContracts` the live `ALLOWED_RPCS` (rpc names, checked against the stubs by `test_guard.py`), as a read.
2. **Where the pyside code lives.** New file `pyside/src/optdesk/ibkr/lister.py`: `ListingGateway(IbGateway)` adds `list_contracts` through the details lane (`DetailsLane.run`: its 20 s timeout and 30 s pause apply, as for `resolve`), formatting every strike with the existing `_decimal_text` (the table's CHECK at `migrations/0032_option_contract.sql:20` is `^[0-9]+(\.[0-9]+)?$` on `strike` text, and the key has `strike` as text, so "12.5" and "12.50" would be two rows); `ibgateway.py` is not edited. **In flight 1:** the lane is used, its width is not (the 2 to 5 lane of single-contract `resolve` would let list calls overlap). `ListingGateway` takes its own one-slot lock (an `asyncio.Lock` in `lister.py`): `await lock.acquire()`, then `try: ... await lane.run(make) ... finally: lock.release()`, the `try` around the whole `lane.run` call. `lane.run` awaits `_admit()` before it creates the request (`pacing.py`: `_admit` raises `TimeoutError` after 20 s, and holds starts for the 30 s pause after a timeout), so the `finally` also covers a start the lane never allowed and a caller that is cancelled while it waits in `_admit`; a lock released only by the request would leak in those cases and block every later call. The wait for the lock is outside the lane's 20 s timeout. **What the lock does not give:** the lane does not cancel a timed-out request (`_on_timeout`, `_late_answer`: it stays in flight at the gateway), and the lock is released when `lane.run` raises, so after a timeout the next call may start once the lane allows it (after the 30 s pause, while `_unresolved` is at most 2) with the abandoned request still in flight: peak 1 holds between answered calls, not strictly after a timeout; this is the exposure `resolve` has on the same lane, and it is named, not prevented. A waiting call is bounded by its caller's deadline: the Go call timeout is 25 s (`internal/broker/grpcbroker/dial.go:23`, `defaultCallTimeout`), so a call queued behind another client's call counts its wait toward the 25 s and may end as a timeout (after a timeout the job waits 30 s and two in a row stop the run, Decision 4); with the one Go worker only a second client can do this, and it is named here, not prevented. So two `ListContracts` rpcs, from a re-run overlapping a run or from any second client, queue and never overlap. Evidence: option-trader ADR 0003:83 and #241 (calls per (underlying, expiry) with strike and right open are safe when serialised, about 5 s each) and #776/#757 (148 issued concurrently made the gateway stop answering). The Go side is serial too: `Run` makes one call at a time (Decision 4, no goroutine), and the scheduler has one worker goroutine (`internal/runner/sched/ticker.go:52-56`), so the pre-open list and the option-open jobs never overlap on main; dividends and bars share the details lane but are not under the list lock (the jobs are serial, so only a non-Go client could overlap them), so the lock is the second guard, on the side that every list caller shares. New file `list_rpc.py` (under 200 lines): validation, the answer, the proto conversion. A protocol `ListerGateway` in `model.py`, as `AccountGateway` and `ExecutionsGateway`, not a method of `Gateway`, so no fake of `Gateway` breaks. `server.py`'s default `build` is `ListingGateway`. **The edit of `transport.py` is the delegating method and nothing else**: an import, the method on `ContractsServicer` (UNIMPLEMENTED when the gateway has no `list_contracts`; else `self._shared.call("list_contracts", context, list_rpc.answer(...))`) and the word in `WORDS`; at most 15 lines, 771 to at most 786 before `ruff format`; the pull request states the count after formatting, and the entry fails if it is over 800 (`transport.py` is not in `ratchet.json`, which holds only `internal/compute/api.go` at 840, so 800 is its limit; the lock needs no other edit of `transport.py`).
3. **Go side.** `broker.ContractLister` (`ListContracts(ctx, underlying Contract, expiry Day, right, tradingClass string) ([]Contract, error)`, `right` empty for both) beside `Broker`; `Dial` still returns `Broker`; the concrete client implements both. UNIMPLEMENTED from an old service is `broker.ErrNotServed`, declared in `api.go` as a constant of an unexported string type (`type notServedError string` and `const ErrNotServed = notServedError("...")`: declarations only, no `var`, no function); the `Error() string` method of that type is in the new file `internal/broker/errors.go` (not an interface file), named in entry 2's Files; `errors.go` is the first file with a func beside an `api.go` in a root of `internal/broker`: every other row root on main holds `api.go` only, so `api.go` stays declarations only and the package does not (`scripts/arch` classifies only sub-packages, so CI passes); the client returns it inside a Permanent error and `errors.Is` finds it (a comparable constant). Window: calendar `XNYS`, `after_close`, `lateness_hours` 0, so `Window()` returns (`XNYS`, 0, 0) and `windowFor` gives open = session open (09:30 ET) and close = session close (16:00 ET) of day D; a Retryable run is retried, and a missing key expires, against that 16:00 close. `RunBound` 47 minutes (the budget plus a 25 s call, the 30 s wait after a timeout and slack): the deadline is max(close, now) + bound, so for a 06:15 start it is 16:47 on an ordinary day and never cuts a normal run; the job's own budget and `stop_time` are the cuts. Half days and holidays: the start is session open minus 195 minutes and the session open is 09:30 on a half day too, so the start and `stop_time` do not move; a holiday has no session, so there is no key that day.
   - *Postcondition and the end states.* **Session.** A list serves the open of the key day D: `listed_session` is D, and the run rows carry D. **Definitions.** `listed` of a run row is the number of groups of the name with `listed_session` equal to D, recounted from `master.option_list` (never from a counter, so a crash between the `option_list` row and the run row loses nothing). `expected` is the groups of that name to list, computed from `ListChains` when the first run of D reached the name: set once, never recomputed by a re-run, `null` (unknown) until that name's `ListChains` answers. Reasons of a name that ended: `none` = nothing of it is left unlisted (every group of `expected` is listed for D: the reason of a name that is complete); `some_short` (some groups of the name are listed for D and the others ended `short` or `error`, none left unasked: `listed` is above 0 and below `expected`; a name with every group `short` or `error` is `all_short`); `no_expiry_in_window` (one definition, used everywhere in this file: `ListChains` answered with at least one expiry for the name and `ChooseExpiries` kept none of them; `expected` is 0); an empty `ListChains` answer (no expiry at all) is not that, it is `listchains_failed` with `expected` unknown, because a name with no expiry at all is stale or wrong chain data, not a name with nothing in the window. **The job implements `runner.Postcondition`**: true when, in the run rows of D (`OptionListStore.ListRuns`, entry 3), some name has `listed` above 0. This agrees with `verdict()` (`runkey.go:175-185`: with `Err` nil and a Postcondition, `Usable` is ignored and the mark is the Postcondition's answer). `Usable` is set to the groups listed, for the log only. **The job computes `Listed`** after each name by counting its `option_list` rows of D (`Lists(underlyingID)`) and writes it through `PutListRuns`. **One rule for `Err`: when no name has `listed` above 0, the run ends Retryable for every reason except `stop_time` and `unserved`, which end Err nil (the `unserved` end state is below)** (`timeouts`, `listchains_failed` (an empty `ListChains` answer included), `broker_down`, `budget`, `list_max_new`, `all_short`: every group ended `short` or `error`, and every name `no_expiry_in_window`, the guard: nothing stored by anyone; a name that ends `some_short` has listed something, so the run is Err nil); **`unserved` ends Err nil, and the key is missing and expires at the close like a late start, on purpose** (main's per-strike path covers the day, and the run rows and the log name it; it is not `no_value`, which the runner gives only to a run with an `Err` of kind `NoValue`, `verdict()`). **In every other case `Err` is nil, including every end of a run that listed something**: it ended on `budget`, `list_max_new`, `stop_time`, `timeouts` or `broker_down` with groups left, and the day is partial; the key is present (`verdict()`: `Err` nil and the Postcondition true), nothing re-runs it, the groups it did not reach keep their older contracts for D, and design B reads the run rows (`reason`, `listed` below `expected`), not the mark. A Retryable run before the 16:00 close is not counted and is started again on a later turn (`nextAttempt`: `beforeClose && isRetryable`; a run with `Err` set gets no mark whatever the Postcondition says, `verdict()`), and a re-run when `expected` is unknown calls `ListChains` first, paying it again (Decision 9: 45 names at 5.0 s is 3.75 minutes). **The Retryable cycle ends at `stop_time`, not at the close**: the first turn after 07:30 is the late-start run below, Err nil, a counted done run; with nothing listed its Postcondition is false, the key stays missing and expires at the close (16:00; 13:00 on a half day), visible. **Retry timing:** `plan()` orders the keys of a turn by DatasetID, and a turn is submitted at every tick and at every slot start (Decision 10); a failed run is retried at every turn from 06:15 to `stop_time` (the process's own tick grid gives up to 5 ticks in that span, for example 06:16, 06:31, 06:46, 07:01 and 07:16, and a retry is not counted before the close), no other key of the day is due before 07:30 (the first, `fred_series`, Decision 10), but a missing key of an earlier day with an open window is run first (Decision 10). The guard costs one `ListChains` per name per turn (45 x 5.0 s = 3.75 minutes of the one worker) and a retry cannot store more than the first run could, so nothing is stored in those turns; a list of only monthly-only names can legitimately hit the guard (a 25-day window holds at most one monthly of a name, and monthlies are 28 or 35 days apart), but a real list holds daily and weekly names, so only a short or stale list does. **A run that starts after `stop_time` reads the stored rows only and makes no broker call at all (no `ListChains`, no `ListContracts`)**; it writes no new reason over a finished row, and for a name with no row it writes a row with `expected` unknown and reason `stop_time`; it ends Err nil; with something listed (an earlier run of D) its Postcondition is true. **Re-run cost:** a run reads its rows first; a name whose `expected` is known is asked for the groups of that set that have no row listed for D, without `ListChains`; a name with `expected` unknown calls `ListChains` first; a name with reason `no_expiry_in_window` (expected 0) has no `ListContracts` call, except in the guard case, where every name calls `ListChains` again and writes the `expected` of a name that now has expiries (an empty `expected` may be replaced, a non-empty one never). 8. **Expiry choice, `ChooseExpiries`** (a pure function in `list.go`; B applies it in the plan so a list is never taken for an expiry the plan drops). It keeps every expiry that `ListChains` lists for the name whose calendar DTE is in [`dte_min`, `dte_max`] = [21, 45], inclusive, counted in calendar days from D, the key day of the run (the session whose open the list serves). Owner decision 2026-11-11 (ask optdesk-2852): the expiries a seller uses are those 20 to 45 days out, as option-trader does. The words "20 to 45" are read as option-trader's rule that the owner named, so the rule built is [21, 45]: an expiry 20 days out is dropped, one 21 days out kept (the table test of entry 5). Its rule for US expiries is DTE 21 to 45 calendar days, ordered nearest to 30 DTE (option-trader `snapshots/params/plan_v4.toml:1-12`, ADR 0003:81, #240). There is no count cap and no monthly rule: a weekly name has about 4 expiries in the window, a monthly-only name 1 or 2, a daily-expiry name (SPY, QQQ, IWM) 17 to 19 (the weekdays of a 25-day window; the arithmetic of Decision 9 uses 17 and states the effect of 19). The order inside a name is nearest to DTE 30, then the earlier expiry (an order, not a cut); `ChooseExpiries` returns it in that order and a test pins it (entry 5). The call sort of Decision 4 stays by nearest expiry across names; this order is what B uses to rank. **The strike band (k = 1.5 sigma, option-trader `plan.py`) is not applied here:** B applies it when it chooses quotes, so a listing takes every strike of a chosen expiry. **A name with no expiry in the window lists none:** its run row has `expected` 0 and reason `no_expiry_in_window` (the definition of Decision 4: `ListChains` answered with expiries and `ChooseExpiries` kept none; distinct from `none`, a complete name; an empty `ListChains` answer is `listchains_failed`). A run where every name is `no_expiry_in_window` has listed nothing, so it is Retryable and not present (the guard of Decision 4). Held contracts of expiries outside the window are not lost (the held path unions them); watched ones are named in B. `dte_min` and `dte_max` are tomls keys (entry 5).
9. **Arithmetic at 5.0 s, per (underlying, class, expiry), with the window of Decision 8.** Inputs are assumptions, none measured: 45 US stocks, 12 held; 5 daily-expiry names (SPY, QQQ, IWM and the like) at **17 expiries each** (a window of 25 consecutive days holds 17 to 19 weekdays, 25 x 5 / 7 = 17.9; 17 is the assumption, 19 is the sensitivity below); of the other 40, **10 weekly names at 4 expiries and 30 monthly-only names at 1.5 on average**. Typical: 85 + 40 + 45 = **170 requests, 14.2 minutes** at 5.0 s (20.7 minutes at 7.3 s). Worst, all 40 others weekly: 85 + 160 = 245 requests, 20.4 minutes (29.8 at 7.3 s). `ListChains` for 45 names as 5.0 s calls is 3.75 minutes [unmeasured] (5.5 minutes at 7.3 s). So the run takes typical 18 minutes (26.2 at 7.3 s) and worst 24.2 minutes (35.3 at 7.3 s): from a 06:15 start, listed by about 06:33 typical and 06:40 worst at 5.0 s, 06:41 and 06:50 at 7.3 s. With 19 weekdays for each of the five daily-expiry names, 10 more requests (0.8 minute at 5.0 s, 1.2 at 7.3 s): worst 25.0 minutes at 5.0 s and 36.5 at 7.3 s, listed by 06:52. The **45-minute budget** (to 07:00) holds all of these; the margin at the worst case at 7.3 s is 9.7 minutes with 17 weekdays and 8.5 minutes with 19. After the 45 `ListChains` calls are taken out (225 s at 5.0 s, 328.5 s at 7.3 s) the rest of the 2700 s holds 495 `ListContracts` requests at 5.0 s ((2700 - 225) / 5) and 324 at 7.3 s ((2700 - 328.5) / 7.3). The budget is checked between calls, so the last call overshoots it by at most the Go call timeout, 25 s. **One timeout must not become two.** After a lane timeout the lane holds starts for 30 s (`DETAILS_PAUSE_S`, `pacing.py`) and `_admit` gives up after 20 s, so a call made at once after a timeout times out in `_admit` without being sent: one slow answer would count as two timeouts and end the run after about 45 s. The job therefore waits 30 s after a timeout before its next call (`ListDeps.Pause`, a function; nil means a real timer of 30 s, which the `Sleep(` grep does not see; every test that makes a call time out injects a `Pause` that advances the fake clock, not only `TestOneTimeoutIsOneTimeout`), and the stop rule is two consecutive timeouts with that wait between them. A timeout costs up to 25 s plus the 30 s wait, and the budget is checked after the wait. These are IB calls only; Decision 6 states the writes. Held is guaranteed: if all five daily-expiry names are held with 7 weekly held names, 5 x 17 + 7 x 4 = 113 requests = 9.4 minutes at 5.0 s (the 12 held names at 4 expiries each would be 48 requests, 4 minutes). Priority has no bound but the budget. **The arithmetic assumes serial calls (in flight 1, Decision 2): the times above are sums, never divided by a lane width.** Two consecutive timeouts stop the run (`stopAfterTimeouts`, `listed.go:36`; reason `timeouts`, Decision 6). If a run stops on its budget with groups left it is partial and the rest keep older contracts (Decision 4).
10. **The worst chain on the one worker, from the tomls on main, and the start that keeps the 09:35 pull on time.** All times ET on an ordinary day (session open 09:30). **How a slot becomes a turn** (`internal/runner/sched/ticker.go`, `loop` and `waitFor`): the scheduler submits a turn at every tick (the first at start + 10 minutes, then every 15 minutes on a grid from the process's own start, not on :00 or :15) and at every published slot start. A due turn that finds the worker busy is not left to the next tick: it is skipped and tried again after `busyRetry`, 5 s, with the mark not advanced. So a job starts at its slot instant on a free worker, else within 5 s of the worker freeing; a key whose slot is in the future is held (`notDue`, `reconcile.go:258`). A turn runs every missing key that is due, in `plan()` order (DatasetID, then subject), and `Reconcile(day)` walks the session days after the cursor up to today, earlier days first (`reconcile.go:85-110`). **The `run_bound` of a morning job cuts nothing:** the deadline is max(window close, now) + bound (`runkey.go:253`) and the close is 16:00 of the key day (96 hours later for the 96 h windows), the same fact as for this job's 47 minutes (Decision 4).     - 08:00 turn: `master.option_contract.gap` (budget 40 minutes, to 08:40 plus a last call), then `obs.fundamental_fact` (`fundamentals`, no cut).

No clock time is claimed for either: from the log the gap and `fundamentals` take about 6 minutes, so the plan has the time before 09:35.

   **Earlier-day keys run first.** `fundamentals` and `fred_series` have windows that stay open for 96 hours, so a missing or Retryable key of an earlier day is retried on every turn and, because days run in ascending order, before today's keys: it can delay the 06:15 key by its own duration, which no bound limits. It is failure-driven and not new.
   **The start.** The pre-open run ends by 07:01 when it starts at 06:15, the stop time is 07:30, and the first main job after it, `fred_series`, is due at 07:30, so the pass does not touch the 08:00 chain: its margin to the gap's 08:00 turn is about 59 minutes for a run that starts at 06:15, about 87 minutes typical (06:33 against 08:00), and at least 29 minutes for a retry that `stop_time` stops (07:31 against 08:00).
