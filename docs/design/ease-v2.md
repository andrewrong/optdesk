## Decisions

1. Port unchanged, version 2. Register id `macro.ease`, `version = 2` (the number of option-trader's version; optdesk has no version 1, and the retired version 1 is not ported). Display only: nothing votes, offers, sizes or gates on it. Scale 0 (tightening) to 100 (easing). Four inputs, equal weight, each a linear sub-score between two fixed points and clipped to 0..100 (`100 * (figure - at_0) / (at_100 - at_0)`):
   - `net_liquidity`: the change of weekly net liquidity (Decision 3), a fraction; +0.03 is 100, -0.03 is 0; may be 10 calendar days old.
   - `us10y`: `DGS10` today minus `DGS10` 63 observations earlier, in basis points (percent x 100); -75 is 100, +75 is 0; may be 3 weekdays old.
   - `fed_funds`: `DFF` on a session minus `DFF` on the session 126 sessions earlier, in basis points, sessions being the observation dates of `SP500`; -50 is 100, +50 is 0; may be 7 calendar days old.
   - `hy_oas`: `BAMLH0A0HYM2`, percent, newest point; 2.5 is 100, 5.5 is 0; may be 3 weekdays old.
   The value is the mean of the sub-scores present (not the rounded ones), rounded half-even to 2 places; it has no value with fewer than 3 present (min_inputs 3); a missing input is one that is absent, short (fewer observations than its window; for `net_liquidity`, one of the eight summed Wednesdays has no figure) or older than its age. "Weekdays old" counts Monday to Friday after the data day through the as-of day, no holidays (`weekdays_after`). The band is taken on the value after the 2-place rounding, rounded half-up to a whole number: 0..33 tighter, 34..66 steady, 67..100 looser. All arithmetic is exact rationals (`math/big.Rat`), each float64 input first turned into its shortest decimal text (`strconv.FormatFloat(v, 'g', -1, 64)`) so that 3.23 is 3.23 and not its binary neighbour; option-trader does the same with `Fraction(Decimal)`. Own implementation, because no library does a clipped linear scale of several inputs and it is stdlib arithmetic; it sits behind the interface `compute.Ease` (owner's rule) so a library can replace it.
2. Weekly alignment, ported unchanged. Weekly net liquidity of a Wednesday W is `WALCL(W) - WDTGAL(W) - RRP`, where `RRP` is the newest `RRPONTSYD` point in the week (W-7, W] (the series is daily; a week with none has no figure). A Wednesday has a figure only when both `WALCL` and `WDTGAL` have a point dated exactly W (a point dated another weekday is not used). The change at W is the sum of the figures of W, W-7, W-14, W-21 divided by the sum of the figures of W-91, W-98, W-105, W-112 (13 weeks back, same four weeks), minus 1; so one rule decides it: each of those eight Wednesdays must have a figure, and 17 weeks (W-112 to W) is only the span they lie in, not a count of figures required (the Wednesdays between, W-28 to W-84, may lack one and the change is still given); there is no change when any of the eight has no figure or the earlier sum is 0 (the two means are of equal counts, so the ratio of sums is the ratio of means). The figure of the as-of day is the newest Wednesday on or before it that has a change (an older one is then judged by the 10-day age: a Wednesday more than 10 days old makes the input stale). `us10y` and `fed_funds` take the newest observation, or session, on or before the as-of day that has a figure; a session whose `DFF`, or whose earlier session's `DFF`, is missing has no figure and the newest earlier one is used. A no-value point (FRED's dot) is not an observation: it is not counted in the 63 or the 126.
3. Units of the liquidity series, and the vintage of the five new ones: the source normalises to whole dollars. `seriesUnits` becomes a table of `{unit, currency, shift}`: `WALCL` and `WDTGAL` are `ccy_amount`, `USD`, decimal point shifted 6 places right (FRED states "Millions of U.S. Dollars"); `RRPONTSYD` `ccy_amount`, `USD`, shifted 9 ("Billions of U.S. Dollars"); `DFF` and `BAMLH0A0HYM2` `percent` (shift 0, "Percent"). The shift is made on the decimal text of the FRED value before it becomes a float64 (so `0.123` billions is `123000000`, with no float error), and the mapper sets `Currency` for every `ccy_amount` row. `mapping_test.go` learns the full recorded text of a `ccy_amount` series: the recorded text must be exactly "Millions of U.S. Dollars" with shift 6 or "Billions of U.S. Dollars" with shift 9, else the test fails. Weekly net liquidity is then `WALCL - WDTGAL - RRP` in dollars, which is option-trader's figure x 10^9; the change is a ratio, so it is the same number. The same table carries `pinVintage` (true for the five new ids): the mapper sets `RealtimeStart`, `RealtimeEnd` and `SourceRevision` to the observation date, so a second fetch of the same observation is the same key and the writer's `DO NOTHING` makes the window refetch of Decision 4 idempotent. The cost, named: the first value seen for an observation is kept and a later revision of it is not stored (the five series are rarely revised and the score is display only); the seven series already loaded keep FRED's vintage. No contract changes: `ccy_amount` and `currency` exist. Rejected: new unit words `usd_millions` and `usd_billions` (a second additive change to `series_unit`, a migration, and every reader then converts), and storing FRED's raw magnitude with the factor in code (the stored point would not say what it is).
4. Loading the weekly and late series: the fred_series manifest gets `windowed = ["WALCL", "WDTGAL", "RRPONTSYD", "DFF", "BAMLH0A0HYM2"]` and one `lookback_days = 14`. For an id in `windowed` the run fetches `[day - 14, day]` instead of `[day, day]`, in one extra `Fetch` call; the other seven are unchanged. The Thursday release of a weekly value then reaches the run of the next session day and, after a missed week, the one after. One number for all five (the review's simplification): 14 is longer than the daily series need and costs nothing. Every id of `windowed` must be in `series`, and `lookback_days` is 1 to 31. The plan hash includes `windowed` (sorted) and `lookback_days`. The window refetch is idempotent only because of the vintage pin of Decision 3; the test of that is in the sources issue and varies `realtime_start` between two fetches (a fake that returned equal vintages would pass while production duplicated). Not verified here: that FRED returns these values by the Friday 07:30 run (no live call is allowed); if a value arrives later, the figure is missing with `stale_input` until it does, which the page shows as a gap.
5. History: the score needs 17 weeks of `WALCL`, `WDTGAL`; 63 `DGS10` observations; 127 `SP500` sessions with `DFF`; `RRPONTSYD` for the same 17 weeks; so about 270 calendar days. The owner loads at least one year of the five new ids with `ot series-load --series ID --from DAY --to DAY` (`compute-fred-series.md` Decision 3; the owner's word is needed before the window runs it). Until then the figure is missing with `short_history` parts. The job's lookback for the series it reads is `2 * window + 14` days of `lookbackDays` with `window` 126 in `rules.json`: 266 days, enough for 126 sessions (about 183 days), 63 observations (about 95 days) and 17 weeks (119 days) with a margin of at least 40 percent for holidays.
6. Register and hash. One new entry, `macro.ease` version 2, in `docs/conventions/register.toml` (15 keys, `pages = ["macro_page.ease"]`, the glossary term `Ease (macro)`, both added by `compute-ease`), `currency` "Unit decimal, stored as decimal text, rounded half-even to 2 places." (the sentence `TestRulesMatchRegister` expects), `implemented_by` every function of `internal/compute/ease` that can change a score: `(*calc).Ease`, `params` (the fixed points, the ages and the band edges are in its body, not in a data file), `score` (the linear sub-score and the clip), `ratOfFloat` (float to exact decimal), `round2` (half-even at 2 places), `wholeHalfUp`, `band`, `weekdaysAfter`, `netLiquidityChange`, `weeklyNetLiquidity`, `changeBack`, `fedFundsChange`, each written `internal/compute/ease.<name>` (the method as `internal/compute/ease.(*calc).Ease`). Twelve names, so the rounding, the conversion and the weekday count are inside the hash (review point 5b). The existing hash (sha256 of the normalised source of the listed functions, then the canonical JSON of the rule's numbers) needs no tool change: `splitName` of `rules_test.go` takes the package from the name. `rules.json` gains `{"id":"macro.ease","version":2,"convention":"ease_v2","unit":"decimal","scale":2,"window":126,"min_valid":3,"max_age_days":10}` (the other keys zero, `hash` real): `window` is the session lookback of `lookbackDays`, `min_valid` is min_inputs, `max_age_days` is the largest of the four ages and only a bound (each input's age is in `params`). The readings of `macro.ease` are stored by `compute_figures` with the rule hash in `reading_input.code_version`. Only the score is registered; `macro.net_liquidity_13w` and the two basis-point changes are statistics inside it, hashed through it (see Left out). One set of band words, in the register and on the page: `偏紧` (0..33), `中性` (34..66), `偏松` (67..100); option-trader's labels 变紧 / 没怎么变 / 变松 are display text and are not ported (the history line says so).
7. Stored form, by the pattern of `compute-figures.md` Decisions 2, 3 and 9. `name` `macro.ease`, `version` 2, `subject_kind` `market`, `subject` `WALCL` (the FRED id of the first input, as Decision 9 says), `unit` `decimal`, `value` the text of the half-even rounding at 2 places; `Figure.DataDay` is the newest data day among the inputs used and `Observations` the number of inputs used. `parameters`: `band` (`value_tag` `tighter`, `steady` or `looser`; absent when the value is missing) and, per input name, `<input>.data_day` (tag, ISO day), `<input>.figure` and `<input>.score` (numbers) for an input present, `<input>.reason` (tag, one of the five reasons) for one missing. A value missing for fewer than 3 inputs is state `missing_input` with `reason_code` 1 (`no_input`: option-trader's single reason `too_few_inputs`); the reason of each missing input is in its own parameter. The inputs row is `obs.series_point` with the seven FRED ids as keys.
8. The page. `macro_page.Config` gains an optional `Read compute.ReadingFunc`; nil keeps today's gap and the existing tests. With it, `Build` reads `macro.ease` v2, `market`, `WALCL` for the day, and for the day 7 calendar days before. A winner reading with a value gives: `figure` (value to 1 decimal half up, text the whole number half up), `marker_percent` (value to 2 decimals, text the whole number), `neutral_band` `low` 33.5 and `high` 66.5 as `percent_of_track` with texts `33.5%` and `66.5%` (the edges of the band on the value, which is taken on the value rounded to a whole number: a marker at 33.7 is inside the drawn band and is `中性`; the home card `macro_summary` and its fixtures draw 34 and 66 and are left as they are, see Left out), `ticks` `0 25 50 75 100`, `week_change` the difference of the two values as an `IndexPointsFigure` when the earlier day has a value (text with the sign, `+1.2` or `−0.2` with U+2212, as the fixtures), else a `Gap` `no_data` `较上周暂无数据`, `reading_text` and `aria_text` in the wording of `macro-summary/ok.json` with the band word of the stored `band` (tighter `偏紧`, steady `中性`, looser `偏松`, the words of the register); the aria text says `中性带 33.5 到 66.5`. A missing or absent reading keeps the `no_data` gap (`松紧度暂无数据`), and the page data time of `macro_ease` is the reading's `data_day`. `cmd/ot` builds `macro_page` with no `Read` on main (`newMacroPage`, `cmd/ot/wire.go`); issue `macro-page-interface` adds the field first (nil keeps today's output byte for byte), `macro-page-ease` fills the page from it, and issue `macro-ease-wire` passes it, after #553 has added the readings DSN to `cmd/ot`.

Registering it is one more `[[figure]]` and one more job key; it is added when a page cites it by id and version. Not a deferral of the score: the score is complete without it.

- The home card `macro_summary` and its fixtures and `internal/pages/home_page/testdata/macro.json`: they draw the neutral band as 34 to 66 while the macro page draws 33.5 to 66.5 (Decision 8). Aligning them is its own change of a different builder; until then the two views differ by half a point of track at the band edge, named here.
- Storing the figure in FRED's own magnitude with new unit words (`usd_millions`, `usd_billions`): rejected in Decision 3, it adds a second change to `series_unit` and a migration and makes every reader convert.
- Keeping the daily fetch of a single day for the weekly series: rejected in Decision 4, the Thursday release comes after the 07:30 slot, so the Wednesday value would only be read if some later run asked for that day; a window that repeats a fetch costs nothing (the writer is idempotent).
- A data file for the fixed points (`ease_v2.toml` as in option-trader) read at run time: rejected, the hash discipline covers function source and the canonical numbers of `rules.json`; numbers in a file the hash does not read would change a score without a hash change. They are in the body of `params`.
- Putting the score in `compute.Figures` as an eighth method: rejected, it changes an interface that the golden suite `contracttest` runs and every fake of it; a separate small interface changes nothing that exists.
- Making `ease` a method of `internal/compute/figures`: rejected, that package may import only `contracts` and `compute` and holds the stats-based figures; the score has no use of `Stats` or `Rolling`, and its own package keeps its hash set small.
- A holiday calendar for "weekdays old": option-trader counts Monday to Friday and the port keeps it; a holiday can make an input count one weekday older than a trading calendar would, which only turns a figure missing one day earlier. Changing it would be a version 3.
- A change of the fixed points, windows or labels: the score is ported unchanged; a change is a new register version with the advisor's agreement.
- What is not verified. No FRED call was made (the rules allow none, and none is asked of an implementer): the `units` texts of the five ids ("Millions of U.S. Dollars", "Billions of U.S. Dollars", "Percent"), that `WALCL` and `WDTGAL` are dated on Wednesdays (option-trader's code relies on it), and that the Thursday release reaches the run of the next session day; the FRED lag of `SP500` (register says up to 4 sessions) can make the `fed_funds` figure 6 to 8 days old across a holiday, past its 7 days, so that input can go missing on such days (unchanged from option-trader; the score then uses the other three, min_inputs 3). The band words `偏紧` and `偏松` are the designer's choice beside the fixtures' `中性`; they are display text, one set in the register and on the page.
