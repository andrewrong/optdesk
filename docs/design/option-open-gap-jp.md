# Option open, plan B: contracts computed, confirmed by listing, cached valid and invalid for seven days, listed on the spot when the cache lacks them; US and Japan

Author: the optdesk designer (draft)
Owner approved: (pending)
Date: 2026-11-12
Revision: 2 (revision 1 was a JP gap stage with a Postcondition; the owner's own shape and the advisor's review r1 replace it)

Owner decision "b", 2026-11-12 00:33Z: invalid contracts are cached for 7 days and re-checked early when the spot leaves the band they were computed for (Decision 5).
Source: owner ask optdesk-2940, 2026-11-12: "when the stored data is bad, collect on the spot; no one-wrong-step-then-all-wrong". Owner shape, same day: compute the candidate contracts; before the open confirm with IBKR which are valid and which are not; store both with a validity of 7 days; after 7 days check again, and a contract still invalid stays cached as invalid; collect only the valid ones; a contract the cache lacks or whose validity ended is checked on the spot, with no step after step; IBKR's real limit is the concurrent market-data lines, not a daily cap. Advisor review r1 (PR 1750, head 710386a4): NoValue only on a gateway answer; a missing input is never a silent no_value; the CHECK constraint of `option_list_run` forbids a new reason; the Telegram and ops views show a failed key only after the window close. Option-trader advisor (00:15Z): JP options are OSE.JPN, class = stock code, multiplier 100, from the chain definition, never SMART; open slot 09:05 JST; at least 3 strikes per side.
Read on origin/main at db5f0f22 (the JP con_id fix of #1745 and #1749 has landed, `wire_optionopen.go`; the design holds with or without it): `AGENTS.md`, `docs/design/modules.md`, `docs/design/option-open-pull.md`, `docs/design/option-contract-lists.md` (Decisions 4, 6, 8, 9, 10; entries 1 to 6), `migrations/0034_option_list.sql`, `internal/jobs/optionopen/{listed,plan,pull,jp}.go` and the tomls, `internal/store/openquote/api.go`, `internal/runner/reconcile/{reconcile,runkey}.go`, `internal/runner/sched/{gaps,ticker}.go`, `pyside/src/optdesk/ibkr/{pacing,ibgateway}.py`, `cmd/ot/{wire_optionopen,check_scheduler,notify}.go`. "[unverified]" marks a claim not checked in code or docs.

Goal: for a watched name of either market, on a session day, the plan and the pull read contracts that a gateway answer has confirmed (valid) or refused (invalid, remembered for 7 days); a name with no fresh answer is listed on the spot before the plan; a run that cannot get an answer is a retrying key that the owner sees the same morning, never a green no_value.

## What main holds today, against the owner's model

| Owner step | On main |
|---|---|
| compute the candidates | done: `candidates` of the sigma band over the chain (`listed.go`), `ChooseExpiries` and the strike rules of `plan.go` and `band.go`, US and JP (`jp.go`) |
| confirm with IBKR before the open | US: evening `option_open_resolve` (per contract) and the 08:00 ET `option_open_resolve_gap`. JP: the evening `option_open_resolve_jp` only. Listing by expiry (`ListContracts`, `master.option_list`) is designed (`option-contract-lists.md`), its tables and store are on main (`0034_option_list.sql`, `optionopenpg/lists.go`), its rpc, broker method and job are not (entries 1 to 6 of that design, not built; US only, JP "out of this design") |
| store valid and invalid | valid: `master.option_contract` (no end date: a listed contract stays valid until its expiry). Invalid: `master.option_not_listed` with `retry_at` 7 plus or minus 2 days (`retry_days = 7`, `jitter_days = 2` in the resolve tomls); written only for "no security definition" (`classify`, `listed.go`) |
| check again after 7 days, invalid stays cached | done for the per-contract path: the mark is due at `retry_at`, asked again, marked again |
| collect valid ones only | done: the plan reads stored contracts only (Decision 1 of the pull design), the pull sends the planned items |
| check what is missing on the spot | US: the 08:00 gap pass only. JP: none. The plan and the pull never ask, and a stage that lacks its input ends `NoValue` (`listed.go:328` "no stored close", `plan.go:202` and `:209` "no stored close", "no resolved chain"), which `verdict()` turns into a final no_value mark (`runkey.go:176`) that `sched/gaps.go` never shows |

So about four of the six steps exist for the US. The delta of this design: the same pre-open confirmation for JP, freshness of seven days for a listing, the on-the-spot listing of a name with no fresh answer, the rule that only a gateway answer ends a key `NoValue`, and a same-morning view of a key that is still retrying, got by closing the list windows early and not by a new gap kind.

## Decisions

### The model, stated once

1. Each stage reads its stored input; the listing stage is the only stage that asks the broker for what the stored input lacks, and it runs before the plan, at the pre-open pass and again as the on-the-spot pass (Decision 5). Valid and invalid are both stored; collection uses the valid ones only. This sentence is written once, in `docs/contracts/ot/jobs/option_open_resolve_gap.md`, and the other contract files point to it.

### Confirm by listing, not per contract (advisor)

2. One `ListContracts` per (underlying, expiry, class) returns every listed strike (`option-contract-lists.md` Decision 1, one `reqContractDetails` with no strike). Validity is membership in that answer: a strike in it is valid (stored in `master.option_contract`), a computed candidate of a listed expiry that is not in it is invalid for the day. One answer fills the positive and the negative cache of the whole expiry. A lookup of a contract that does not exist, one by one, is the expensive path: it waits the details lane timeout of 20 s and then a pause of 30 s (`pacing.py`: `DETAILS_TIMEOUT_S`, `DETAILS_PAUSE_S`). So the per-contract resolve stays only where it is today (the US evening pass and gap) and is not extended to JP.
3. What the negative cache is. The invalid side is absence from a fresh listed group: no row is written per invalid contract, and the plan reads valid contracts only, so a strike that is not in the list is never planned and never asked. The per-contract marks of `master.option_not_listed` (7 plus or minus 2 days, `retry_at`) stay for the per-contract path and are not written by the list job. Only a gateway answer makes a group fresh: a timeout, no answer, a disconnect, `ErrNotServed` and a `short` list (`option-contract-lists.md` Decision 6, "real list") leave the group unlisted, so nothing is ever cached invalid from them. This is the test the per-contract path already has (`classify` marks `outNotListed` only: an empty answer or "no security definition", `listed.go:153-176`).
4. Freshness, seven days. A group is fresh when `master.option_list` has a row with outcome `listed` whose `listed_session` is on or after the day `D - (list_ttl_days - 1)` (calendar days), so `list_ttl_days = 1` means listed for the session D itself (the daily behaviour of `option-contract-lists.md`, its default) and 7 means D-6 to D. The list job's `listed` recount and its end reasons count a fresh group as listed, so a group listed on D-3 is not `not_finished` on D. The valid cache has no seven-day end: a listed contract cannot stop being valid before its expiry, so re-asking costs lookups and finds nothing. The invalid cache ends with the freshness of its group: after the days the group is listed again, a strike still absent stays invalid, a strike now present becomes valid. `list_ttl_days` is a value of the list job's own manifest parser (`list.go`). Jitter: the days of a group are `list_ttl_days` plus a draw in minus to plus `list_ttl_jitter_days` (2), a pure function of (underlying, expiry, class) (a hash modulo 5, minus 2), so a test is exact and no column is needed; it keeps the groups of the first day from all falling stale on the same morning. Prior art: option-trader kept a not-listed cache of 7 days with plus or minus 2 days of jitter.
5. Early re-check when the spot leaves the band (owner decision "b", 2026-11-12). The cache is seven days, and a fresh group is re-listed at once when the target zone computed from the newest spot is no longer inside the range the group was confirmed for: a strike that IBKR adds after a big move is then seen at the next pass, not after seven days. The band, the zone and the test are Decisions 14, 15 and 19; they need the confirmed range stored with the group (`BandLo`, `BandHi`, the in-band count and the sigma source, entries 1 and 2), and the test runs in the morning passes (entry 4). A group with no stored band (listed before this change) or a name with no spot is treated as stale: it is listed, so nothing is trusted that cannot be proved inside its range.
6. The on-the-spot pass: the list job code runs a second time with a manifest of its own at the pass before the plan (US 08:00 ET, JP 07:30 JST), `gap_only = true`, its own dataset and one key (`master.option_list.gap`, subject `gap`; JP `master.option_list.gap_jp`; the pre-open JP pass `master.option_list_jp`, subject `preopen`; so no key is dropped as the duplicate of another job's, and each dataset has its own window, Decision 11): it lists the groups that are not fresh (a name added late, a group the pre-open pass did not finish, a stale group), nothing else, in the order of need of `option-contract-lists.md` Decision 4 (held names, then priority names with a buy price, then the rest; inside a class the nearest to 30 DTE for the US and to the middle of 14 to 90 for JP, then the earlier expiry; never by symbol alone). A name with every group fresh costs no broker call, as the `ListChains` of today's gap does not (it asks once per name; the list job reads `master.option_list` first and asks `ListChains` only for a name that has a stale group).
7. JP. The same job code, a JP rule set (`jpRules`, `jp.go`: exchange OSE.JPN, multiplier 100, class = stock code, `N225` as the index of class 225, the names without options excluded), a JP expiry window of DTE 14 to 90 (`job_plan_jp.toml`), and two slots on XTKS: `option_list_preopen_jp` at 06:30 JST and `option_list_gap_jp` at 07:30 JST with `stop_time` 08:10 JST so that it ends before the plan at 08:15. Whether the pyside `ListContracts` serves OSE names is unverified (Doubt 1): if it does not, every name ends `unserved`; the list design ends such a run with `Err` nil and a false Postcondition, a counted run that is never re-run and shows at the close only (`option-contract-lists.md` Decision 5), so the JP and gap manifests set `unserved_retryable = true` and the job returns `Retryable` for `unserved` instead; the key retries and its window closes early (Decision 11). JP then stays on the evening per-contract resolve of today.

### NoValue only on a gateway answer; a missing input is never a silent no_value

8. The rule, for resolve, gap, list, plan and pull, US and JP: a key ends `NoValue` only when a gateway answer proves the market has nothing (an empty chain for the name, an expiry list that is empty after a real answer, "no security definition"). A missing stored input is not such an answer. The cases on main: "no stored close" (`listed.go:328`, `plan.go:202`): `Retryable` with the text "no stored close of X", re-run until the window close, because the bar of the day may arrive later; "no resolved chain" (`plan.go:209`): `Retryable` (the on-the-spot pass fills it before the plan; a name that truly has no options is `no_option` or ends `expired` at the close, shown, not silent); the pull's "no plan" (`errNoPlan`, `pull.go`): `Retryable`, since the plan job is the stage that failed; "no con_id" is already `Retryable` in `listed.go:307` and the list job takes the same line. `no_option` names and names off the watch list stay `NoValue`: the owner's own words are the answer.
9. Retry mechanics, from the code. A `Retryable` run before the window close is not counted (`reconcile.go:349`) and is run again at every turn: every 15 minutes (`sched/ticker.go:32`) and at every slot instant, until the close of its dataset window. A clean result with `Usable` 0 is already `empty_before_close` and retried the same way (`runkey.go:189-193`), so no read-back and no `Postcondition` method is added: a false `Postcondition` is a counted run that is never re-run (`runkey.go:182-188`, `reconcile.go:349-357`), the opposite of what is wanted. Each `Retryable` return writes one line `fault stage=<id> day=<day> key=<key> reason=<text>` through the job's `Log`, so a stage stuck all morning leaves one line per 15 minutes. A re-run with nothing to ask costs no broker call (Decision 6 reads the lists first).
10. The JP con_id: the fix has landed (`wire_optionopen.go`, #1745 and #1749), and the design does not depend on it: a JP name with con_id 0 is a `Retryable` key of the list stages (`listed.go:316` has the same line) and of the gap, visible by Decision 11.

### Same-morning visibility

11. What shows a failed key today: `sched/gaps.go` `keyKind` gives a missing key a gap only after the window close of its dataset (`!closed || running` gives none, except the crash brake), and a `no_value` key none unless the runner wrote `expired`. The readers of open gaps are `ot check scheduler`, the Telegram notifier (`NotifyGaps`, which prints a reason it does not know as "unknown", `internal/notify/core/text.go`), the morning notice and the freshness read; no web page lists the gaps of the day (`pages/gaps.Lines` has only test callers) [unverified beyond `grep` of non-test callers]. So the same-morning view is made with the existing kind `expired`, not a new one: the list datasets have their own windows (Decision 6) and the list job states a window that closes at its stop time, `close_offset = "-7h20m"` of the session close (US 16:00 gives 08:40 ET, the plan is at 08:45; JP 15:30 gives 08:10 JST, the plan is at 08:15; the JP pre-open pass `-8h30m` gives 07:00 JST). A key still retrying at that instant ends `expired` through the Close table, retries stop at the plan, and `expired` is a gap that Telegram and `ot check scheduler` already report. A page that lists gaps is not in this design (Left out, Doubt 2).

### Load: IBKR's limits, and which limit bounds which stage

12. There is no daily cap and no per-name count in this design. The limits, from the code that enforces them and from IBKR's documented numbers:
   - Message rate: the TWS API allows 50 messages per second by default [unverified: IBKR documentation, from memory]; `MessagePacer` of the pyside transport holds 40 starts per second for every message (a cancel counts), `DEFAULT_STARTS_PER_SECOND` in `pacing.py`. It binds everything on the one connection, and is far above what the lookups need.
   - Contract-detail style requests (`ListChains`, `ListContracts`, `ResolveContract`, dividends, bars) go through `DetailsLane`: in flight 2 at the start, rising to at most 5 when ten answers in a row average under 1 s, halving when ten answers average over 3 s; no new start while the oldest request in flight is older than 5 s (`_OLDEST_MAX_S`); a request that waits 20 s times out, drops the limit to 1 and pauses starts for 30 s; more than 2 unanswered requests block new starts. The Go client of these jobs dials with `ResolveInFlight: 1` (a choice of `wire_optionopen.go`, so the lane's rise to 5 is not used; Doubt 8) and a call timeout of 25 s (`wire_optionopen.go`, `broker.Options`). The probe quoted in `option-contract-lists.md` found list calls finishing in 5.0 s steps with no overlap [unverified: probe files are not in the repository], so one list request is taken as 5 s. This lane bounds the pre-open pass, the gap pass and the on-the-spot pass.
   - Concurrent market-data lines: 100 by default per IBKR account [unverified: IBKR documentation]; `LineBudget` holds 70 lines and 50 snapshots in flight (`DEFAULT_LINES`, `DEFAULT_SNAPSHOTS`) and shrinks on error 101. This bounds the pull and the refresh button, not the lookups; the lookups subscribe to no market data.
   - Worst case, JP, from these: 20 watched JP names [assumption] with 4 expiries in the window is 80 list requests at 5 s: 400 s, under 7 minutes, in the first pre-open pass; a typical gap pass lists only the stale groups. A sick gateway: each request can cost 20 s plus a pause of 30 s, 50 s; the run stops at two timeouts in a row (the rule of `option-contract-lists.md` Decision 4 and of `resolveAll`, `listed.go`), so at most about 100 s are lost before the stage ends retrying; and the stage's own clock bounds the rest: `stop_time` 08:10 JST for the gap, `stop_time` 07:00 JST for the pre-open pass. The US figures are those of `option-contract-lists.md` Decision 9 and are not changed.
   - Worst-case pre-open duration at one listing in flight and 5 s per listing (option-trader's figure for an open-strike listing: about 5 s per (underlying, expiry), 33 names by 3 expiries in about 8 minutes, #776; our probe also gave 5.0 s steps [unverified]). First day, no group fresh, today's one window (US 20 to 45): 3 indices x 50 groups + 30 stocks [assumption] x 2 groups = 210 listings = 1050 s, 17.5 minutes. Adding the 7 to 14 and the 180 to 400 windows: 3 x (50 + 16 + 16) + 30 x (2 + 2 + 6) = 246 + 300 = 546 listings = 2730 s, 45.5 minutes: over the 45-minute budget of the pre-open key, so the first day of a wider window runs over two mornings (the key is `Retryable` at the budget, not counted, and the next pass continues; a stale group is listed in the order of need, Decision 6). A later day lists about one group in seven (the jitter spreads them) plus the new expiries of the day: for the three windows about 80 listings, 400 s. The early re-check adds only the expiries whose zone left the range.
   - The turn against the US evening resolve. One worker runs one key at a time, in the order of DatasetID and then subject (`reconcile.go:183-190`), and a busy worker retries a due slot after 5 s (`ticker.go`, `busyRetry`). US `option_open_resolve` runs from 17:00 ET with a budget of 40 minutes (`job_resolve.toml`), so to about 17:41 ET with the last call. 07:30 JST is 17:30 ET in winter and 18:30 ET in summer. In winter the JP gap waits for the worker until about 17:41 ET (07:41 JST); its `stop_time` of 08:10 JST then leaves 29 minutes for work that needs under 7 in the first pass and much less after it. Because the stop is a clock time and not a duration from the start, a late start cannot push the gap past the plan at 08:15. In summer there is no overlap of the gap slot; the JP pre-open slot of 06:30 JST is 17:30 ET in summer, inside the US resolve, so it starts when the worker frees (about 17:41 ET, 06:41 JST) and its `stop_time` of 07:00 JST leaves 19 minutes for a first pass that needs under 7. A retried gap key runs before the plan key of the same turn because `master.option_list` sorts before `plan.option_open_item`; the pull keys of a turn are those of the plan rows read at the start of the turn, so the pull never runs in the turn that stores the plan.

### The band, the windows and the strikes

14. The band, per expiry, no DTE buckets: strikes K from `spot*exp(-band_k*s)` to `spot*exp(+band_k*s)`, with `s = sigma*sqrt(DTE/365)` and `band_k = 1.5` (manifest). Delta (US 0.15 to 0.30, JP 0.10 to 0.35) is the pull's filter after the quotes and is never used to build the band. Floors, also in the manifest: the band reaches at least `band_floor_pct = 0.10` of spot on each side (lo at most `0.90*spot`, hi at least `1.10*spot`), and holds at least `min_strikes_side = 3` listed strikes on each side. It is a new function of the list job, `ListBand`, beside `Band` and `StoredBand` (`planfunc.go:161-171`); the per-contract resolve keeps `StoredBand` (`store_k` 1.75 and `store_drop` 0.25 of `job_plan.toml`) unchanged. There is no upper cap of the 25 percent kind: a listing costs one request whatever the band, so the width decides only how many listed contracts the plan can use, and the pull's delta filter bounds what is collected (Doubt 11 for the stored rows of a wide band).
15. The zone and the early re-check. The target zone of an expiry from a spot S' is, per side, `S'*exp(-1.05*s)` to `S'*exp(-0.5*s)` below and `S'*exp(+0.5*s)` to `S'*exp(+1.05*s)` above (`zone_near = 0.5`, `zone_far = 1.05`, manifest). When the zone is not inside the confirmed range `[BandLo, BandHi]` of that expiry, that expiry is listed again, one listing, at the next pass, in the order of need, one in flight, inside the pass's `stop_time`; a pass that cannot finish leaves the rest stale for the next pass, so a vol spike or a gap day cannot grow the pre-open run past its stop time (the zone widens with sigma, so a spike can make many groups stale at once: Doubt 15). The room is `band_k - zone_far = 0.45` of `s`: at sigma 0.30 and 30 DTE that is about a 4 percent move down [computed: 1.5 s = 0.129, 1.05 s = 0.090, difference 0.039]. The test runs in the morning passes (the gap pass and the JP pre-open pass; spot: Decision 17). It does not run in the pull: the pull takes its open spot and cuts its band in `core.go:174-185` from stored plan items (`pull.go:295`) and writes no plan rows, so a relist there would never be quoted, and `core.go` is not a file of this design. That is reported, not edited (Left out); the pull is protected by the morning check, which uses the newest stored close. Prior art: option-trader re-listed on a 5 percent move, on a new strike in the chain, and on its 7-day cache with plus or minus 2 days; the new strike in the chain is not used here (a chain call per name before the open is the cost this design avoids), the 5 percent is replaced by the zone test, and the jitter is added (Decision 4).
16. Sigma, one rule for both markets: the larger of the stored ATM implied vol (the `prior` else `last` of `Stored.sigmas`, `inputs.go:150`, the newest `option_eod` day within `priorSigmaDays`/`sigmaLookback`; `iv_history` is the same stored source) and the 20-day realised vol of the stored daily closes of the name. If both are missing the sigma is `DefaultSigma` 0.30 (`planfunc.go:33`) and the source `default` is stored with the group (`SigmaSource`: `iv`, `rv20`, `default`). Never a live IV before the open. Whether a stock's `rv20` is stored is unverified (main stores `market.rv20` for SPX, NDX and RUT only, #1677 #1679); if it is not, the list job computes it from the 21 stored closes, as one small function behind the same reader, not a new store (Doubt 12).
17. Spot before the open: the prior close from the stored bar, for US and JP alike. If the stored bar is missing the list job asks the gateway for the last daily bar on the spot (`HistoryClient`, `internal/broker/api.go:250`, one name, one request) and, if that fails too, the key is `Retryable` (Decision 8), never `NoValue`.
18. No strike is generated from an assumed grid. One `ListContracts` per (underlying, expiry, class) returns the real grid, whatever its step: 2.5 or 0.5 near the money on a short-dated expiry (a 497.5 strike is simply in the answer), coarse steps on LEAPS. The candidates of an expiry are the listed strikes inside the band plus, when fewer than `min_strikes_side` lie on a side, the nearest 3 listed strikes of that side. The negative entry is the group row: outcome `listed`, the band, `InBand` 0 (expiry E had no strike in the band on day D); a `short` or `error` outcome is never such an entry (Decision 3). One listing is in flight at a time (`ResolveInFlight: 1`), because option-trader overloaded the gateway with open-strike listings at 148 expiries a day (#776).
19. The windows are a list. The list job's DTE windows are a manifest list `[[window_dte]]` of `min_dte`/`max_dte` pairs; today one entry per market (US 20 to 45, the plan's `min_dte`/`max_dte` of `job_plan.toml`; JP 14 to 90, monthly), and adding two-week (7 to 14) or long-dated (180 to 400, LEAPS) is a manifest edit, not code. Decisions 14 to 18 hold for every window: `sqrt(DTE/365)` narrows a short-dated band and widens a LEAPS band, and the 10 percent and 3-strike floors keep a short-dated band from collapsing. This design builds the list job to take the list; it does not make the plan, the pull or the resolve read it (they read the stored contracts by their own `min_dte`/`max_dte`, and widening them is the entry that follows the owner's choice of windows, Doubt 10).
20. Listings per name per window, the unit of load (Decision 12): one per (expiry, class) in the window. A stock with monthly expiries: 1 or 2 in 20 to 45 DTE, 3 to 4 with weeklies [assumption: from the usual expiry calendar, not read from a chain]. SPX, NDX and RUT, daily expiries and a second class each (SPX and SPXW, NDX and NDXP, RUT and RUTW) [unverified: class names from memory]: about 25 expiries and 50 groups in 20 to 45 DTE, about 8 expiries and 16 groups in 7 to 14, about 16 groups in 180 to 400.
