Goal: for a watched name of either market, on a session day, the plan and the pull read contracts that a gateway answer has confirmed (valid) or refused (invalid, remembered for the life of the group); a group with no fresh answer is listed before the plan; a second list slot before the US open relists the expiries whose target zone, from a live pre-open spot, left the confirmed range; a run that cannot do its work, and a job that is not wired, is never a silent no_value or a silent absence and is on a page the same morning with its reason code. One list job serves both markets.

## What main holds today, against the owner's model

| Owner step | On main |
|---|---|
| compute the candidates | done: `candidates` and `ChooseExpiries` (`listed.go`, `plan.go`, `band.go`), US and JP (`jp.go`) |
| confirm with IBKR before the open | US: the evening `option_open_resolve` (per contract) and the 08:00 ET `option_open_resolve_gap`. JP: the evening `option_open_resolve_jp` only. Listing by expiry is designed in `option-contract-lists.md`: its rpc is merged (entry 1, #1751), the store interface and the tables (entries 3 and 4, migration 0034) are on main; the broker method (entry 2), the job (entry 5) and its wiring (entry 6) are not built |
| store valid and invalid, 7 days | valid: `master.option_contract`, no end date. Invalid: `master.option_not_listed` marks, 7 plus or minus 2 days (`retry_days`, `jitter_days`), written for an answered "not listed" only |
| re-check after 7 days, still invalid stays invalid | the mark's `retry_at` per contract; no band, no early re-check |
| collect only the valid ones | done: the plan reads `ListOptions` (`plan.go:205`) |
| check what is missing on the spot | none before the open for a name with no stored contract; the plan is a no_value or a Retryable, the pull has no key when the plan is empty (Decision 10) |

So about four of the six steps exist for the US. The delta of this design: the cache (7 days, band, early re-check) in the one list job, JP as a second manifest of that job, the silence at the empty plan, the pull re-check, and a page of today's failures.

## Decisions

### The model, stated once

1. Each stage reads its stored input; the list job is the only stage that asks the broker for what the stored input lacks, and it runs before the plan; valid and invalid are both stored and collection uses the valid ones only. There is no second job family: the job `option_list_preopen` of `option-contract-lists.md` (Decision 4) is built with the freshness and the band of this design (this pull request amends its Decision 4, its "real list" rule and its entry 5), and the JP side and the second US slot are further manifests of the same job code. The jobs `option_list_gap`, `option_list_gap_jp` and the keys `gap_only` and `unserved_retryable` of revision 2 are gone: a list key whose held or priority groups are not fresh is `Retryable` before its window close, so it is run again at every turn (Decision 6).

### Confirm by listing, not per contract

2. One `ListContracts` per (underlying, expiry, class) returns every listed strike (`option-contract-lists.md` Decision 1). Validity is membership in that answer: a strike in it is valid (stored in `master.option_contract`); a computed candidate of a listed expiry that is not in it is invalid. The count of requests is the count of groups, never of contracts.
3. The negative cache is the group, not a row per strike: the plan reads valid contracts only, so an absent strike is never planned and never asked. Only a gateway answer makes a group fresh: a timeout, no answer, a disconnect, `ErrNotServed` and a `short` list leave it unfresh, so nothing is cached invalid from them (the test the per-contract path has, `classify`, `listed.go:153-176`). An answer that is real but empty for an expiry that the chain lists (the gateway answered, nothing is listed) is stored as outcome `listed` with `Count` 0 and is fresh for the seven days like any other group: it is not a daily gap and not a daily re-ask. This amends the "real list" rule of `option-contract-lists.md` Decision 6, which made an empty answer `short`; the risk, a transient empty answer cached for seven days, is Doubt 6, and the early re-check of Decision 5 is its only guard. The per-contract marks of `master.option_not_listed` stay for the per-contract path and are not written by the list job.
4. Freshness, seven days, no jitter (the owner said 7; the plus or minus 2 days of option-trader are prior art, Doubt 14). A group is fresh when `master.option_list` holds a row with outcome `listed` whose `listed_session` is on or after the day `D - (list_ttl_days - 1)` (calendar days): `list_ttl_days = 1` is the daily behaviour of `option-contract-lists.md` (its default) and 7 means D-6 to D. The `listed` recount and the end reasons of that job count a fresh group as listed, so a group listed on D-3 is not `not_finished` on D. The valid cache has no seven-day end (a listed contract cannot stop being valid before its expiry); the invalid cache ends with the freshness of its group. A group whose band was built with the default sigma (`SigmaSource` `default`) is fresh for the rest of that day only: the next list pass relists it, because a band of an assumed sigma is not a confirmation worth seven days. All groups listed on one day fall stale on the same morning seven days later, so the load of every seventh day is the first-day load (Decision 19), bounded by the budget and the stop time; a long weekend adds nothing, the days are calendar days. The effective life of a group is shorter than seven days for a volatile name: at sigma 0.30 and 30 DTE the room (0.45 s, about 3.9 percent) is crossed by a daily move of about 1.9 percent in about (3.9 / 1.9)^2 = 4 days, so about a quarter of the groups, about 75 listings and about 6 minutes at 5 s, are relisted every day [computed from the assumed sigma, not measured].
5. Early re-check when the spot leaves the band (owner decision "b"; asks 2940 and 2962): a fresh group is listed again when the target zone computed from a newer spot is no longer inside the range the group was confirmed for (Decisions 12 and 13). The range is stored per group, that is per underlying and expiry, with its time (`BandLo`, `BandHi`, `Sigma`, `ListedAt`). This is how "invalid contracts are cached 7 days" is met: a strike inside the stored range that is absent from the listing is invalid, and stays invalid until the group is listed again (seven days at most, one day for a default-sigma band, or earlier when the zone leaves the range); a strike outside the range is not invalid but unasked, so the plan, which reads valid contracts only, never asks it. The check runs at each list pass on the newest stored close and at the second slot on a live pre-open spot (Decision 13). A group with no stored range, or a name with no spot, is stale.
6. On the spot, from the same job: with a freshness above one day, a run ends `Retryable` while a held name or a priority name (`Stored.Watch` with a `BuyPrice`) has a group neither fresh nor answered, even when other names listed. This amends `option-contract-lists.md` Decision 4 in this pull request (its Err rule made a run `Retryable` only when no name listed anything; with a seven-day cache a partial run would then be present and never run again, and the stale groups of the held names would stay behind the cache). The runner runs a `Retryable` key before its window close at every 15-minute turn and every slot instant (`reconcile.go:349`, `ticker.go`), so a group that failed at 06:15 is asked again at the next turn, in the order of need (held names, priority names, then the rest, nearest expiry first), and a fresh group costs no broker call; a stale group of any other name is listed at the next day's pass and does not hold the key, so a name whose group never answers does not make the key retry all day. After `stop_time` the job makes no broker call. A name added to the watch list after the key is present is listed by nothing before the plan (Doubt 5).
7. JP: the same job code, a JP manifest (`list_jp.go`, `job_list_preopen_jp.toml`): the JP rule set `jpRules` (`jp.go`: exchange OSE.JPN, multiplier 100, class = stock code, `N225` as the index of class 225, names without options excluded), the JP expiry window (DTE 14 to 90), calendar XTKS, zone Asia/Tokyo, dataset `master.option_list.jp`, subject `preopen`. It cannot run before the open: the runner's turn day is the UTC date (`ticker.go:557`, `dayOf`), so the key of Tokyo day D is first planned at 00:00 UTC of D, which is 09:00 JST (06:30 JST is 21:30 UTC of the day before, and the walk of that turn holds the session of D-1); the existing JP plan at session open minus 45 minutes has the same property on main [by code reading, not run]. So the JP manifest starts at the session open (`session_open`, offset 0) with `stop_time` 09:20 JST, before the pull's `stop_after` 09:30, and the JP plan and pull of that turn run after it because their dataset ids sort after `master.option_list.jp` (`reconcile.go:183-190`) [unverified]. The JP list has no second slot: a slot before the JP open belongs to the previous UTC day and has no key (this decision), and a slot at the open would run after the JP plan of the same turn; JP has no pre-open re-check (asks 2940 and 2962 are met for the US only; 3015 and 3021 are the late-collection asks): a JP gap at the open is repaired by late collection under the design that follows asks 3015 and 3021 (the owner on 3015: losing the open is fine, collect late until the next session opens, do not be rigid), and the next day's list pass tests the zone from the new close; if the JP list runs past the 09:05 JST pull slot the pull runs late, which ask 3015 allows once late rows are marked. The evening JP resolve (`option_open_resolve_jp`) stays as it is: it is what the JP plan reads at 09:00 on a day when the list has not served. A list before the JP open needs the ticker to plan the next day's session early, a runner change that is not in this design (Left out, Doubt 16). JP names are never SMART and never guessed: class, exchange and multiplier come from the chain definition. Whether the pyside `ListContracts` serves OSE names is unverified (Doubt 1).

### Silence: a missing input is never a silent no_value

8. The rule, for list, resolve, plan and pull, US and JP: a key ends `NoValue` only when a gateway answer proves the market has nothing; a missing stored input is never such an answer. The cases on main: "no stored close" (`listed.go:328`, `plan.go:202`), "no resolved chain" (`plan.go:209`) and "not on the stock list" (`plan.go:155`) end `NoValue` today and become a typed error with a stored reason code (Decision 10; `Retryable` only for `fetch_failed`) ; the exception is a name whose list group is fresh `listed` with `Count` 0 or whose list reason is `no_expiry_in_window`: that is the gateway's answer and the plan ends `NoValue` for it, so a watched stock with no options is not a daily gap (the plan reads the list rows through a `ListRows` port that `PlanDeps` gains, entry 3, wired in entry 8); "wrong day" (`pull.go:218`, `jp.go:257`) is not a data fault and stays; "no con_id" is already `Retryable` (`listed.go:317`, #1746; the JP con_id fix has landed, #1745 and #1749, and the design holds with or without it). A `Retryable` run before the window close is not counted (`reconcile.go:349`), is run again at every turn and writes one line `fault stage=<id> day=<day> key=<key> reason=<text>` through the job's `Log`.
9. A clean result with `Usable` 0 is already `empty_before_close` and retried (`runkey.go:189-193`), so no `Postcondition` method is added to the existing jobs: a false `Postcondition` is a counted run that is never re-run (`runkey.go:182-188`, `reconcile.go:349-357`). The list job of `option-contract-lists.md` has its own and keeps it.
10. The silence where it is born, and its reason. Today a plan key is one per stock (`plan.go:97`), so an empty plan has no key at all, and the pull `Plan` returns no keys and no error when the day's plan is empty (`pull.go:176-186`): no expected key, no run, no gap, nothing on any page (the pull of 2026-11-11). A `Plan` error would only be a Warn line (`planLogged`, `reconcile.go:149-165`). A sentinel key was tried and dropped (a false `expired` gap at the dataset close once the plan appears). So: each plan job has one fixed key per market (dataset `plan.option_open_item` and subject `us` for the US, dataset `plan.option_open_item.jp` and subject `jp` for JP, the `.jp` form of the registered bug issue `optionopen-jp-own-keys`) and its `Run` plans every stock. A run that stores no item ends with a typed error and a stored reason code from an enumerated set: `no_con_id`, `no_close`, `no_chain`, `no_list`, `no_stock_list` (the cause of each name), written to `ops.option_run_reason` (entry 2; the runner's `capture_run` row holds only the error kind, `runkey.go:172-196`, and the error text goes to the log). The run is `Retryable` only for a transient failure of the on-the-spot fetch of a missing close (reason `fetch_failed`); `no_con_id`, `no_close` after a good fetch, `no_chain` and `no_list` are `Permanent` for the day, counted and not re-run: the next list pass or evening resolve, not this key, is what fixes them, and the page shows the code, so nothing is silent. Per name, not per run: the run writes one reason row per failed name (scope = the symbol) whenever any name fails, also when other names were stored, so one failed name of 45 is on the page; a name with no stock-list row is `no_stock_list`. The end of a run: no error when at least one item was stored; `Retryable` when every failure is `fetch_failed`; otherwise `Permanent` for the day. A key that ends `Retryable` is run again at every turn before the close of its own dataset window (Decision 11). The pull's own empty plan keeps returning no key (the truth: nothing to pull), and the page explains it by the code of the plan row of the same market.

### Same-morning visibility

11. What is seen today: `sched/gaps.go` `keyKind` gives a missing key a gap only after the window close of its dataset, a `Retryable` key before the close is no gap and a `no_value` key none unless the runner wrote `expired`, so a failed morning key reaches Telegram at the close, hours after the open; no page lists the runs of the day, `ops.capture_run` has no `rd` view and no reader for the page role, and a scheduled job that `ot serve` leaves out when a key is missing (on 2026-11-11 from 10:59 to 11:50 +08: "job not wired: OT_PG_DSN_OPTION_OPEN is not set") has no key, no run and no gap, a second silent state beside the empty plan. The owner works on the web page, so the same-morning view is one page, `ops_today_page`, with one section "today" and one kind of row: the newest run of each market key with its reason code, and, for each job that is not wired, a row `not_wired` with the NAME of the missing key (never a value). The rows are written by the scheduler's wiring (`withWiring` is called by the scheduler, run-once, backfill and notify, `runonce.go:172,282`, `backfill.go:58`, `notify.go:53`; `ot serve` wires no jobs), through the ledger store that `withWiring` already opens with the runner DSN before any job is built (it exits if that fails, so the write can always happen), under one fixed day-less key (dataset `ops.not_wired`, subject `scheduler`, scope = the job id, the key name in the detail), and each scheduler startup replaces the whole set, so a row never outlives its fix. The page shows only the datasets of this design (`plan.option_open_item`, `obs.option_open`, `master.option_list`, `master.option_contract` and their `.jp` forms) and the `not_wired` set, with the market taken from the dataset id. A row is: dataset, key, market, status, error code, reason code, returned and usable counts, and the producer's `detail` text (for example the underlyings and expiries then outside the range, Decision 13). Nothing else is on the page: no gap list (`pages/gaps` is not importable from a pages sub-package, `modules.md`), no ranking. The list job sets `Usable` (the groups listed), so a clean run that listed nothing is a row with 0. An early window close for the list datasets was tried and dropped: on a US half day (close 13:00 ET) `-8h30m` is 04:30 ET, before the 06:15 start; `Window()` stays (`XNYS`, 0, 0).

### The band, the windows and the strikes

12. The band, per expiry, no DTE buckets: strikes K from `spot*exp(-band_k*s)` to `spot*exp(+band_k*s)`, `s = sigma*sqrt(DTE/365)`, `band_k = 1.5` (manifest). Delta (US 0.15 to 0.30, JP 0.10 to 0.35) is the pull's filter after the quotes and never builds the band. Floors, in the manifest: the band reaches at least `band_floor_pct = 0.10` of spot on each side (lo at most `0.90*spot`, hi at least `1.10*spot`) and holds at least `min_strikes_side = 3` listed strikes on each side. `ListBand` is a new function in `list.go`, the file of the list job (entry 5 of `option-contract-lists.md`), beside the `Band` and `StoredBand` of `planfunc.go:161-171`, which are not edited; the per-contract resolve keeps `StoredBand` unchanged. There is no upper cap of the 25 percent kind: a listing is one request whatever the band, and the pull's delta filter bounds what is collected (Doubt 11).
13. The second slot before the open (asks 2940 and 2962), a manifest of the list job code, not a job family: `option_list_zone`, dataset `master.option_list`, subject `zone`, calendar XNYS, anchor `session_open`, offset `-75m` (08:15 ET), `stop_time` 08:40 ET, mode `zone`. It is before the plan slot (08:45 ET, `job_plan.toml`) because a contract listed after the plan would never reach the plan. It reads a live pre-open spot of each name that has a group with a stored range (the gateway snapshot; Doubt 20: whether a usable pre-open spot exists, else the last trade with its age, which the slot then stores in the detail text), computes the target zone of each expiry from that spot (per side `S'*exp(-1.05*s)` to `S'*exp(-0.5*s)` below and `S'*exp(+0.5*s)` to `S'*exp(+1.05*s)` above, `zone_near = 0.5`, `zone_far = 1.05`, with the stored `Sigma`) and relists only the expiries whose zone is not inside `[BandLo, BandHi]`, one listing in flight, held names first, inside its own budget and `stop_time`; the groups it relists are stored as in the preopen pass (range, sigma, time). The room is `band_k - zone_far = 0.45` of `s` (about 4 percent at sigma 0.30 and 30 DTE [computed]). What is not covered, stated and accepted: a move between that slot and the open is not re-checked, and an expiry left outside the range by a failed or cut listing keeps the stored plan; the zone run writes, for each such underlying, one reason row `outside_range` (scope = the underlying) with its expiries in the detail text, so the today section names them; a name with no usable spot gets `no_spot`, and a run that starts after its `stop_time` lists nothing and writes `late_start`, so the zone slot is never silently absent. The zone mode writes `option_list` groups and reason rows only: it does not write `option_list_run`, whose per-name `listed` and `reason` the 06:15 pass owns (`putListRunSQL` overwrites them). The key is present whenever it ran; the reasons are what show a zone run that did nothing. The spot port is `broker.Broker.Snapshot(ctx, Kind, []Contract)` (`internal/broker/api.go:101`), reading `Last`, `Bid`, `Ask`, `Close`, `ReceivedAt` and `MarketDataType` of the `Quote`, a read-only `Snapshotter` type in `ListDeps`. The pull never lists and never writes plan items.
14. Sigma, one rule for both markets: the larger of the stored ATM implied vol (`Stored.sigmas`, `inputs.go:150`: the implied vol of the stored contract nearest the spot on the newest `option_eod` day) and the 20-day realised vol of the stored daily closes. On a first listing a name has no stored contract, so `Stored.sigmas` returns 0 and 0 (`inputs.go:151-153`): the IV leg is absent exactly when the band is first needed, so a first band uses the realised vol of the stored closes (21 closes of `price_bar`, one small function behind the same reader; main stores `market.rv20` for SPX, NDX and RUT only, #1677 and #1679; JP close coverage is unverified), and without it `DefaultSigma` 0.30 (`planfunc.go:33`) with the source `default` stored (`SigmaSource`: `iv`, `rv20`, `default`). Never a live IV before the open. Then the 10 percent and 3-strike floors set a first band that does not depend on the sigma being right (Doubt 13).
15. Spot before the open: the prior close from the stored bar, US and JP alike. Order: freshness first, then the bar only for a name that has a stale group or whose range is to be tested; a missing bar is asked of the gateway once (`HistoryClient`, `internal/broker/api.go:250`, the last daily bar, one request) and, if that fails too, the key is `Retryable`, never `NoValue`.
16. No strike is generated from an assumed grid: one `ListContracts` per (underlying, expiry, class) returns the real grid, whatever its step (2.5 or 0.5 near the money on a short-dated expiry, so 497.5 is simply in the answer; coarse on LEAPS; 16.67 alternating 17 and 16 on one JP name, `option-capture-jp-stocks.md`). The candidates of an expiry are the listed strikes inside the band plus, when fewer than `min_strikes_side` lie on a side, the nearest 3 listed strikes of that side. The group row stores `BandLo`, `BandHi`, `Sigma`, `SigmaSource` and `InBand`; `InBand` 0 is the negative entry "expiry E had no strike in the band on day D". One listing is in flight at a time (`ResolveInFlight: 1`) because option-trader overloaded the gateway with open-strike listings at 148 expiries a day (#776).
17. The windows are a list because the owner will want others: the list job's DTE windows are a manifest list `[[window_dte]]` of `min_dte` and `max_dte`, today one entry per market (US 21 to 45, the window of `option-contract-lists.md` Decision 8; JP 14 to 90); a two-week window (7 to 14) or LEAPS (180 to 400) is a manifest edit, and Decisions 12 to 16 hold for each (`sqrt(DTE/365)` narrows a short band and widens a LEAPS band, the floors keep a short band from collapsing). The plan, the pull and the resolve read the stored contracts by their own `min_dte` and `max_dte`; widening them is the entry that follows the owner's choice of windows (Doubt 10).

### Load: IBKR's limits

18. There is no daily cap and no per-name count of our own. The limits, from the code that enforces them and from IBKR's documented numbers:
   - Message rate: the TWS API allows 50 messages per second by default [unverified: IBKR documentation, from memory]; `MessagePacer` (`pyside/src/optdesk/ibkr/pacing.py`) holds 40 starts per second for every message, a cancel counts. It binds everything on the one connection and is far above a list run.
   - Contract-detail style requests (`ListChains`, `ListContracts`, `ResolveContract`, bars) go through `DetailsLane`: in flight 2 at the start, at most 5 when ten answers in a row average under 1 s, halved when ten answers average over 3 s; a timeout sets the limit to 1 and pauses 30 s; a 20 s timeout; more than 2 unresolved block new starts; no new start while the oldest request in flight is older than 5 s (`_OLDEST_MAX_S`). The Go side dials these jobs with `ResolveInFlight: 1` (`wire_optionopen.go`) and `CallTimeout` 25 s. A probe gave 5.0 s per list request [unverified]; `option-contract-lists.md` Decision 9 uses 7.3 s.
   - Concurrent market-data lines: 100 by default per account [unverified]; `LineBudget` holds 70 lines and 50 snapshots in flight and shrinks on error 101. This bounds the pull and the refresh, not the lookups.
19. The counts and the worst-case times, one listing in flight, 5.0 s per listing [one probe; unmeasured on this host]. US, today's window, 45 names: the figure of `option-contract-lists.md` Decision 9 is 36.5 minutes at 7.3 s for 12 held names and 5 daily-expiry names, about 300 listings, so 25.0 minutes at 5.0 s [computed: 36.5 x 5 / 7.3]; its 45 daily `ListChains` calls are in that figure. The first day, and every seventh day when all groups fall stale together, runs from 06:15 ET and ends by about 06:40 at 5 s or 06:52 at 7.3 s; its budget is 45 minutes and its `stop_time` 07:30 ET. A day with ordinary traffic relists the groups of the new expiries, the default-sigma groups and the groups whose zone left the range: about a quarter of the groups, about 6 minutes (Decision 4) [computed, unmeasured]. The zone slot lists the expiries outside the range at 08:15 ET: at most the same groups, a few on a quiet morning, at 5 s each inside 25 minutes (08:15 to `stop_time` 08:40); the single worker may still be in the 08:00 gap job (budget 40 minutes, measured about 5.6 minutes, `option-contract-lists.md`), so the worst case is a zone run that starts after 08:40 and lists nothing, which it reports as `late_start`. JP: seven names with options (`option-capture-jp-stocks.md`, four expiries each on the day it probed), the window 14 to 90 DTE holds about 2: 14 groups, 28 at most, 70 to 140 s, from 09:00 JST with `stop_time` 09:20 JST (Decision 7); the JP pull slot is 09:05 JST (`job_pull_jp.toml`, offset 5m), so a JP list that runs past 09:05 holds the single worker and the pull starts when it ends, at 09:20 at the latest, before the pull's `stop_after` 09:30. The windows not built (SPX, NDX and RUT with daily expiries and a second class each, about 82 listings per index [assumption: expiry calendars from memory, class names SPXW, NDXP, RUTW unverified]) add about 410 s per index: the owner's later choice (Doubt 10).

## Doubts

1. Whether the pyside `ListContracts` serves the OSE names (class = stock code, OSE.JPN, multiplier 100) is unverified. The dispatcher probes port 4001 once entry 1 of `option-contract-lists.md` (#1751) and the pyside restart are in (read-only; never the account port 4002). Consequence if it does not serve them: every JP name ends `unserved`, which `option-contract-lists.md` Decision 5 ends with `Err` nil and the key unmarked; that key expires at the window close and is a gap, the runs page shows it the same morning, the JP plan reads the evening resolve's contracts as it does today, and JP stays on the evening per-contract resolve until the rpc serves OSE.
2. The page of entries 6 to 9 is a new page: `web/` has none to extend. Its nav slot is a "Runs" link in the `TopNav` menu; whether the owner wants a main item is the owner's.
3. The seven days are a group freshness here, not a per-contract date, because one listing answers a whole expiry. The valid side ends at the contract's expiry, not at seven days; the valid ones are refreshed when the group goes stale. Owner call if valid contracts must also be re-listed on a fixed seven days.
4. IBKR's numbers (50 messages per second, 100 market-data lines) are from memory [unverified]; the 5.0 s per list request is a probe, `option-contract-lists.md` Decision 9 uses 7.3 s; the code limits (pyside `pacing.py`) are verified.
5. The list key is one key per market and day: a name added to the watch list after the key is present is listed by nothing before the plan, the plan is `Retryable` for it and the next day lists it. Owner call if a later pass is wanted.
6. A transient empty answer for an expiry the chain lists is cached seven days (Decision 3); the early re-check is its only guard. Owner call if an empty answer should be confirmed by a second ask before it is cached.
7. `store()` in `listed.go` gives every option instrument `CalendarID` XNYS, also for JP; whether that is right for OSE instruments is unverified and not changed here.
8. The Go client of these jobs dials with `ResolveInFlight: 1`, which keeps the lane at one request and is itself a bound the owner did not set; whether the list job dials with the default of 5 is left to entry 10 and the probe.
9. The 7 plus or minus 2 days of the per-contract marks (`jitter_days`) stay for the per-contract path; the list job has no jitter (Decision 4).
10. Which DTE windows are wanted (two-week 7 to 14, LEAPS 180 to 400, others) and for which names is the owner's later choice; this design makes them configuration of the list job only (Decision 17); the index names multiply the listings (Decision 19).
11. A LEAPS band is wide (sigma 0.30 and 365 DTE: 0.64 to 1.57 of spot) with no 25 percent cap, so the contract rows stored per expiry grow; the number of rows per expiry on a coarse LEAPS grid is unverified.
12. `rv20` of a stock is not stored on main; the list job computes it from stored closes (Decision 14); JP close coverage is unverified.
13. The first band, per 100 of spot, when a name has no stored contract and so no IV (Decision 14); sigma is the realised vol of the stored closes if there are 21, else the default 0.30. A US name at the default 0.30: 21 DTE (the window's first day) gives s = 0.072 and the band 89.8 to 111.4 (1.5 s = 0.108); 30 DTE gives 87.9 to 113.8; 45 DTE gives 85.4 to 117.1; at an rv20 of 0.15 and 30 DTE the formula gives 93.8 to 106.7 and the 10 percent floor sets 90 to 110. A JP name at the default 0.30: 14 DTE gives 91.6 to 109.2, widened by the floor to 90 to 110; 30 DTE gives 87.9 to 113.8; 90 DTE gives 80.0 to 125.0. The real figures for the owner's names (7 JP names with options, `option-capture-jp-stocks.md`) need their stored closes and are not read here; the JP grid steps (250, 100, 25 and 16.67 on one name) are the listing's to give, and whether 3 listed strikes lie on each side of a band at those steps is unverified.
14. Prior art, option-trader (via its advisor), and where this design differs: k = 1.5 for every expiry and both markets (`plan.py:128-144`), the first strike on each side of spot always kept (the intent of the 3-strike floor); its fallback without a sigma was a plain band of spot x (1 plus or minus 0.30), not a sigma of 0.30 in the formula (here 0.30 enters the formula with its reason stored: a choice, narrower on short expiries); its sigma was the underlying's 30-day IV from IBKR (tick 106) of the newest stored quote (here the larger of the stored ATM IV and rv20: a choice); "at least 3 strikes per JP side" was never built there; its listing refresh widened the band by 0.10 x spot per side and 3 days of DTE (`QUALIFY_MARGIN`), which matches the 10 percent floor (the 3 days are not taken); it kept a not-listed cache of 7 days with plus or minus 2 days of jitter and a re-ask on a 5 percent move or a new strike in the chain (here the zone test, no jitter, no chain call); cost on record: about 5 s per (underlying, expiry), 33 names by 3 expiries in about 8 minutes, and 148 expiries a day overloaded the gateway (#776). Nothing is on record of a wanted strike being missed.
16. JP lists at the open (Decision 7), not before it, so on a JP day the list and the JP plan of the same turn are ordered only by the dataset ids; if the plan ran first it would read the evening resolve's contracts. The remedy is a ticker change (plan the next session before its UTC day) that no entry here carries. A busy turn can also run the plan first (`ticker.go:230-240`): the cost is one JP day of the old data after a gap, shown on the page as the row of the list, not a silent day.
18. The US and the JP pull share one dataset id and one key space on main (`obs.option_open`, `batch-NNN`, `jp.go` overrides only `Run` and `runKey`), and the two plan jobs share `plan.option_open_item`; `putWindows` takes one window per dataset from the first sorted key, so on a day with both sessions one market's close may govern the other's keys, and one market's `batch-001` may be shadowed by the other's (`reconcile.go:290-306`, `planLogged` dedups keys by value). The registered bug issue `optionopen-jp-own-keys` gives the JP pull its own key dataset and a window; entry 3 gives the JP plan and the JP resolve theirs. The pull's positional `batch-NNN` names stay (a grown batch shifts the later names; a plan that changes after the keys exist replaces the expected set by its hash, Decision 15's finding of revision 4). This may be a live fault on main today, a cause of the 2026-11-11 silence; the dispatcher checks it read-only on `ops.capture_run` rows of `obs.option_open` for 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11 (unverified).
19. `plan()` computes the keys of every job at the start of a turn, so the JP pull has no keys in the 00:00 UTC turn and is picked up at the next turn (Decision 7 says the order inside the turn; the next turn is the first the pull can run). A slow JP list that runs to 09:20 leaves the pull about 10 minutes of its 09:30 stop.
20. Whether a usable pre-open spot exists at 08:15 ET (Decision 13): the probe is one read-only snapshot of a few names on port 4001 at 08:15 ET (never 4002), reading the bid, ask, last and the age of each; if there is none, the last trade with its age is used and the age is stored in the detail text; if even that is old (more than a day), the zone check is skipped for the name and the group stays on the prior-close check. The zone slot is at 08:15 ET and not 09:00 because the plan runs at 08:45 ET: the advisor and the owner may move the plan slot instead.
21. The scheduler not running at all is the remaining silent state: nothing writes `not_wired` then, and #1724 (`ot setup pg` logging missing keys) is the other half.
23. A failed read of the stock list or an empty watch list ends the plan without a key: `listedJob.Plan` (`cmd/ot/listedjob.go`) reads the underlying list and the held names before the inner `Plan` and returns an error with no key when the read fails or the list is empty, and `planLogged` then logs one Warn line. This design does not change it (the file is in no entry): the consequence is that these two states stay log-only; it is that job's fault to fix.
22. The effective life of a group (about 4 days at sigma 0.30, about 6 minutes of relisting a day) is computed from an assumed daily sigma and is unmeasured.
