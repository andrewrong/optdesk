## Goal

After each XNYS close, `bars_refresh` stores the daily bars of every name on `watch.toml` and of four indices (and, weekly, the adjusted_last history), `fx_refresh` stores the day's USD.JPY mid, and `compute_figures` stores the own HV30 reading (`market.hv30_own`) of each underlying that has option rows. This design stores numbers and readings only. No page, schema or builder is edited, so the pages still print their gaps until the designs named under `## Left out` read the stored values.

## Decisions

1. Producers in this design: the store tables and their readers, `bars_refresh`, `fx_refresh`, the HV30 step of `compute_figures`. Pages, scoring, the scanner and the report blocks are other designs.
2. Source of the bars: IBKR historical data through pyside, one new rpc `Bars` on the existing `MarketDataService` (unary, one contract, one kind, daily bars, an optional end day). The plan names IBKR bars; the glossary defines the index closes as IBKR `TRADES` daily bars; Stooq is dropped by the owner (Left out). The rpc and the servicer method are owner-only paths (header). The Go side is a separate `broker.HistoryClient`, as `ReferenceClient` is, so `broker.Broker` and its fakes stay unchanged.
3. Two bases, never mixed in one series: `trades` (IBKR `TRADES`, split-adjusted by IBKR, not dividend-adjusted; this is the basis of HV(n) in `GLOSSARY.md`) refreshed every run; `adjusted_last` (IBKR `ADJUSTED_LAST`, split and dividend adjusted) re-pulled in full once a week (Decision 4). `adjusted_last` has no reader in this design (returns and the 200-day line come with the pages that need them); it is stored because IBKR restates it and the history cannot be rebuilt later. `BarKind` of the rpc also has `MIDPOINT`, asked only by the FX job; the rpc is one for all daily bars, while `obs.price_bar` accepts only the two bases and the FX answer goes to `obs.fx_rate`.
4. `adjust_epoch` (alignment: "weekly adjusted_last re-pull with adjust_epoch"). Rows are insert-only. A row key is (instrument_id, basis, adjust_epoch, session_day). The epoch of a (instrument, basis) starts at 1; the winner rows (what readers return) are those of the highest epoch of the pair. `trades`: a 5-day pull each run; when a pulled day that is already stored differs from the stored winner close by more than `restate_tolerance` (relative, exact `math/big.Rat`; default 0.00001, window default), the job pulls the whole backfill window and inserts every row of that pull under epoch + 1 in one call (one transaction, so a half pull never wins); otherwise it inserts only the days not stored. A split shows up at once as such a difference. `adjusted_last`: unconditional, no diff test (cadence of 7 days: owner 2026-11-09): when the newest stored winner row of the name was received 7 or more calendar days before the run day (or none exists), the job pulls the whole window and inserts all of it as epoch + 1. Why the simple form: IBKR restates the whole adjusted history at every ex-dividend date, so a diff test could only reproduce what a full weekly re-pull gives, and an ex-date of any age up to 7 days is picked up by the next pull; the price is one request per name per week and about 275 rows per name per week of storage, insert-only (the old epochs stay, never read). Between pulls the adjusted history is up to 7 days stale; nothing reads it yet (Left out).
5. The session day of a bar is the exchange date that IBKR gives (`formatDate` 1), stored as it comes; there is no time-zone conversion and no join to `master.session_day`. A JP name therefore has its own JP dates and a US holiday has no US row.
6. Universe: every `WatchEntry` of `watch.toml` (US and JP stocks; the owner puts held names there, the earnings job reads the same file) plus the job manifest tables `[[index]]` for SPX, NDX, RUT and N225 (the four rows of the market page; owner 2026-11-09). The instrument id is the one `option_eod` uses: `ResolveInstrument` with the identifier `ibkr_conid`, so an underlying's bars and option rows share one id. An index entry whose `con_id` is 0 is resolved once per run through `broker.Broker.ResolveContract`; not listed is Permanent for that key.
7. Depth, units and pacing. The first pull of a name asks for `backfill_days` = 400 calendar days (about 275 sessions: more than the 200-day line and one year as ADR 0003; owner 2026-11-09: first 400 days, then 5 years); a daily pull asks for 5. The rpc carries `duration_days` and pyside maps it to IBKR's units: `N D` for 365 or fewer, else whole years rounded up (`N Y`; 400 days is `2 Y`; the job keeps every row the answer holds); IBKR refuses daily bars over 365 D. `ADJUSTED_LAST` is asked with an empty end date (IBKR refuses it with one); the request has an optional `end_day` (`YYYY-MM-DD`, empty means now), pyside sends an empty end date when it is empty and the end day in IBKR's end-date text otherwise, and an `end_day` with `ADJUSTED_LAST` is INVALID_ARGUMENT. IBKR paces historical requests at 55 per 10 minutes and refuses an identical request within 15 seconds (ADR 0003): pyside keeps one pacer on the injected clock, waits up to 300 seconds and then answers `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`; the Go client maps that to Retryable and its call timeout `HistoryTimeout` is 330 seconds (a new `broker.Options` field). One name is one runner key. The first backfill is resumable: at most `backfill_names_per_run` names (10; owner 2026-11-09) start a first pull per day; a name beyond the cap answers with no request and no error (an empty answer, which the runner retries before the window closes and which starts over the next day), so `run_bound` holds whatever the watch list size, and a 49-name list is complete in 5 days at worst. Deepening to 5 years (owner 2026-11-09), in pieces: the rpc keeps `duration_days` 1 to 730, so deepening is not one request of 1825 days. Once a name's 400-day backfill is complete, the weekly step (with the `adjusted_last` pull) deepens its `trades` when the oldest stored `trades` day is newer than the run day minus `deepen_days` (1825) plus 30 days: it asks a piece of at most `deepen_piece_days` = 730 days that ends on the day before the oldest stored day (`end_day`), inserts the rows under the current epoch, and asks the next piece the same way until the oldest stored day reaches the target or a piece holds no older day (1425 missing days are 2 pieces). A piece overlaps no stored row, so it has no diff test; a split is caught by the next daily 5-day pull (Decision 4), which re-pulls the 400-day window as epoch + 1, and the next weekly step deepens that epoch again. `deepen_names_per_run` is a manifest value, 0 meaning no cap (owner 2026-11-09: the first pass is capped at 10, the deepening is not; the run may then exceed `run_bound` and the runner's retries finish it). A name listed for less than 5 years is asked at most once a week (the answer holds no older day), so there is no daily retry. `deepen_names_per_run` and `deepen_piece_days` are manifest values. `adjusted_last` is not deepened (nothing reads it; it stays at `backfill_days`). Schedule: XNYS, `session_close` plus 10 minutes, `run_bound` 10 minutes per key (owner 2026-11-09; `compute_figures` runs at plus 20 minutes and reads whatever is stored then, with its `data_day`). The wire shape (bar size, durations, empty end date, date format) has not been seen against a real gateway: entry 6 carries a recorded-shape fixture written from the IBKR documentation, and the window records one real answer once after merge and replaces the fixture (no live broker in any test).
8. Tables, schema `obs`, one migration, two store packages (declarations `internal/store/bar`, implementation `internal/store/barpg`), one role `ot_w_bar` (line `OT_PG_DSN_BAR`): `obs.price_bar` (typed columns: `instrument_id`, `session_day`, `basis`, `adjust_epoch`, `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `volume`, `currency`, `source`, `received_at`; prices as decimal text, no float) and `obs.fx_rate` (`pair`, `rate_day`, `rate`, `source`, `received_at`). Views `rd.price_bar_r1` (winner rows) and `rd.fx_rate_r1` for the roles `ot_w_bar` (SELECT; the job reads through the view) and `ot_ro_pages` (SELECT; the figures job and pages read with the pages DSN, as the series store is read). No UPDATE, DELETE or TRUNCATE for anyone but the owner.
9. FX: pair `USDJPY` only (the plan's JPY exposure; no other pair is asked for). `fx_refresh` asks IBKR for the daily `MIDPOINT` bar of the IDEALPRO cash contract USD.JPY (resolved through `Broker.ResolveContract`) for 5 days after each XNYS close and inserts the close as the rate of the bar's date, `source` `ibkr_midpoint`, a conflicting key left as it is. There is no hard-coded or default rate (ADR 0004): `LatestFxRate(pair, asOf)` returns the newest row on or before `asOf` and a found flag; no row is "FX unknown", and what age is too old is the consumer's rule. Where IBKR cuts the FX day is IBKR's convention, unverified (as the glossary says for XAUUSD). The 15 s stream and the ADR 0004 shadow period and ECB cross-check are not built (Left out).
10. HV30 producer (the "producer feeding the existing HV function"): a step of `compute_figures`, after IV30 and before the market figures, with the reading name `market.hv30_own` (GLOSSARY.md "HV30", "HV(n)"), subject kind `underlying`, one reading per subject the job already lists (the underlyings with option rows on the day). Rule `market.hv30_own` v1: convention `hv30_v1`, unit `decimal` (an annual fraction: 0.31 is 31 percent), scale 6, `n` 30, `trading_days` 252, `max_age_days` 4, implemented by `HistoricalVol` (so no hashed body changes). Input: the winner `trades` rows of the underlying with `session_day` between `asOf` minus 70 calendar days and `asOf`, ascending, close text to float64 at the job's edge (the precedent of `ivinput.go`); fewer than 31 closes is a missing reading `short_history`, none is `no_input`, a stale newest close is `stale_input`, never a zero. IBKR's own HV30 (`Quote.HistoricalVol`) is not read here and is never a fallback. A new field `PriceBars bar.PriceBarReader` of `jobs.ComputeStores`; nil means the step is skipped and the job behaves as today.
11. Own code behind an interface: the broker client is `broker.HistoryClient` (a library or another gateway can replace it); the restatement rule and the pacer are small glue and live inside their package (`restate.go`, `history.py`), reached only through the job and the servicer. Library check: `math/big` of the standard library for exact compare (no decimal library in `go.mod`, as `option-candidates.md` Decision 1); no new direct dependency.
12. Owner 2026-11-09 (the questions A to E of revision 1): A weekly `adjusted_last` re-pull; B the four indices SPX, NDX, RUT, N225; C first 400 days, then 5 years (Decision 7); D the slot close plus 10 minutes; E first pass at most 10 names a day, deepening uncapped (a manifest value, 0 = no cap). Permanent intraday quote storage: not now, under Left out. Still window defaults, one manifest value each: `restate_tolerance` 0.00001 and the pacing figures of Decision 7.
